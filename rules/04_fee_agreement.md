# Rule 04: Fee agreement analysis

Read for these, in order:

1. Is there a written agreement at all? Rule 1.5(b) requires the basis or rate of the fee to be communicated, and a number of states add a reasonableness factor that turns on whether the agreement was in writing. Check section 2 of the state pack before naming one. If there is no written agreement, say so plainly, because it strengthens the client's position rather than weakening it.
2. What is the fee structure? Flat, hourly, contingent, or hybrid. Quote the client's own document back to them, not the rule.
3. What rate, for whom? Many agreements set different rates for partner, associate, and paralegal time. Check the invoice against the right one.
4. What expenses are the client's responsibility, and are they charged at cost or with a markup?
5. Does the agreement say anything about changing the rate? Rule 1.5(b) requires changes in the basis or rate to be communicated.
6. Is there a scope limit? Work outside the agreed scope that was never separately agreed to is a communication issue and a reasonableness issue at once.
7. For contingent fees specifically, check that the agreement is signed by the client, states the percentages at settlement, trial, and appeal, identifies which expenses are deducted, and says whether the fee is calculated before or after expenses. Check whether the client ever received the closing statement.

Output the result as a ledger, not prose.
