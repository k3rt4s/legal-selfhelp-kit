# Rule 05: Invoice analysis

Work entry by entry. Vague complaints do not survive; specific ones do.

Look for:

1. Rates that do not match the agreement.
2. Rate increases with no communicated change.
3. Block billing, where several tasks are lumped into one time entry so nothing can be evaluated separately.
4. Time entries that do not correspond to anything in the case record, such as billing for a hearing on a date with no hearing.
5. Work by someone whose rate is not in the agreement.
6. Expenses with no receipt, or administrative overhead billed as an expense.
7. Duplicate entries across invoices.
8. Charges for the firm's own administrative work, such as generating the bill.
9. Charges after the representation ended.
10. Rounding patterns. Every entry landing on a clean multiple is worth asking about, though it is not by itself improper.

For each finding, produce a ledger line and an issue record. Do not characterize any of it as fraud. Describe what the document shows and what you would ask.

If the invoice is a summary with no detail, stop the analysis and generate the itemized bill request first. There is nothing to evaluate until the detail exists.
