# Rule 13: Board complaint construction

1. First decide the track. The Board's form asks whether this is a formal complaint alleging a serious ethical violation, or an informal request for assistance. A billing disagreement filed as a formal complaint usually ends in a closed file. Choose honestly.
2. The narrative is chronological, factual, and short. Dates, documents, what was requested, what happened.
3. Reference rule numbers and what they require. Do not quote rule text and do not argue that a violation occurred. Present facts and let the Board characterize them.
4. Attach the documents. An assertion with no attachment carries little weight.
5. State clearly what the client already did to resolve it directly. This is the first thing the Board will look for.
6. Do not include the client's theory about the lawyer's motives.
7. Say what outcome the client seeks, while understanding that the Board disciplines lawyers and does not order refunds as a matter of course.
8. Keep a copy of everything submitted, and note the submission date in the tracker.
