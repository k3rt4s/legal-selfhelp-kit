# Rule 13: Disciplinary complaint construction

1. First decide the track, and check section 4 of the reader's state pack for what the tracks
   actually are. Some disciplinary authorities offer both a formal complaint and a lower-key
   request for assistance; others take one intake for everything. Where both exist, a billing
   disagreement filed as a formal complaint usually ends in a closed file. Choose honestly.
2. The narrative is chronological, factual, and short. Dates, documents, what was requested,
   what happened.
3. Reference rule numbers and what they require. Do not quote rule text and do not argue that
   a violation occurred. Present facts and let the authority characterize them.
4. Attach the documents. An assertion with no attachment carries little weight.
5. State clearly what the client already did to resolve it directly. This is the first thing
   an intake reviewer looks for.
6. Do not include the client's theory about the lawyer's motives.
7. Say what outcome the client seeks, while understanding that a disciplinary authority
   disciplines lawyers and does not order refunds as a matter of course.
8. Keep a copy of everything submitted, and note the submission date in the tracker.
