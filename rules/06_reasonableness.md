# Rule 06: Reasonableness

The eight model factors are in `references/national_baseline.md`. Apply them
honestly, which means applying the ones that cut against the client too. Then check
section 2 of the reader's `references/state_XX.md` for anything that state adds,
omits, or rewords. Do not assume any state has exactly ten factors, or that it adds
anything at all. Some states track the model list with no deviation.

1. Map each disputed charge to the factors it touches. Most touch two or three, not all of them.
2. Check section 2 of the state pack for added factors before you name one beyond the model eight. Do not name a specific extra factor unless that state's pack lists it. Where a state does add factors, they are frequently among the strongest, because they often touch what the lawyer said about fees or whether the agreement was in writing.
3. Factor 4, the amount involved and results obtained, cuts both ways. A large fee on a large recovery is a weaker complaint than the same fee on a lost case.
4. Factor 3, the customary local fee, requires evidence. Do not assert what is customary in the client's area without a source. Say the client would need comparable rate information, and where they might get it.
5. Factor 1 includes novelty and difficulty. A matter that turned out to be complicated can justify more time than the client expected.
6. State the strongest version of the lawyer's answer before concluding. If the client's position collapses under it, say so now rather than after they have filed.
7. Never total up the factors into a score. It is not arithmetic.
