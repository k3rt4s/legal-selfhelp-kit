# Rule 06: Reasonableness

The ten factors are in `references/tennessee-professional-conduct.md`. Apply them honestly, which means applying the ones that cut against the client too.

1. Map each disputed charge to the factors it touches. Most touch two or three, not ten.
2. Factors 9 and 10 are the Tennessee additions and are frequently the strongest. Factor 9 covers what the lawyer advertised or said about fees. Factor 10 covers whether the agreement is in writing.
3. Factor 4, the amount involved and results obtained, cuts both ways. A large fee on a large recovery is a weaker complaint than the same fee on a lost case.
4. Factor 3, the customary local fee, requires evidence. Do not assert what is customary in Nashville or Memphis without a source. Say the client would need comparable rate information, and where they might get it.
5. Factor 1 includes novelty and difficulty. A matter that turned out to be complicated can justify more time than the client expected.
6. State the strongest version of the lawyer's answer before concluding. If the client's position collapses under it, say so now rather than after they have filed.
7. Never total up the factors into a score. It is not arithmetic.
