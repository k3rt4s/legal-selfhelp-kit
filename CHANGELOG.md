# Changelog

All notable changes to legal-selfhelp-kit, in plain English.

## 2026-08-27 (1.0.0)

Shipped. The version number says the scope is closed, not that the kit is finished being correct. It covers all fifty states and the District of Columbia, every legal claim in it has a verification row, and nothing further is planned beyond the twice-yearly source sweep in `MAINTAINING.md`. 0.1.0 and 0.2.0 were never tagged and are not being tagged now; this changelog is the record of what shipped when.

- Corrected `rules/01_scope_and_limits.md`, which limited a stated deadline to sections 5 and 7 of the reader's state pack. Most packs carry their fee arbitration filing window in section 3, the program section, so the rule as written barred the model from stating the most time-critical fact in the pack: Alaska's twenty-day stay of a fee suit, Arizona's three-year arbitration window, and the equivalent in every state that runs a program. `llm/SYSTEM_PROMPT.md` already had the correct formulation, so the two layers were contradicting each other. The rule now allows section 7 or the specific program section that names the deadline, and requires the section to be named when the deadline is stated.
- `scripts/validate_tracker.py` reports a row that spills past the last column instead of validating it clean. An unquoted comma in the notes cell pushed the text after it into a field the validator does not require, so the row passed and the note was silently truncated on export.
- `scripts/check_sources.py` resolves a backreference to the source the row names rather than to whichever URL happened to come last. A row reading "Same Chapter 9 source, Rule 9.112" means the Chapter 9 row, which is not always the row above it, and interleaving one unrelated source between them used to send every following row to a page it never cited. The checker then reported the claim as missing from a source the row does not use. Resolution now reads a named host first, the literal word "above" as adjacency, then a document descriptor matched on whole words in an earlier row's prose and URL path, and only then a section or rule number for a row that offers nothing else. A row naming a host no earlier row in the file carries is reported as unparseable instead of being given a URL by guesswork.
- Thirty-six rows across eight verification files changed the source they are checked against as a result, and every one was re-checked by hand against the page it now names. Twenty Michigan rows moved from an order adopting a single rule to the rules book they cite. The rest, in Alabama, the District of Columbia, Indiana, Kentucky, Mississippi, New Mexico and Oregon, moved onto the host each row names. The parsed and unparseable counts did not move, so no coverage was traded for the correction.
- Wrote down what the first full source sweep proved about the checker's blind spots. `THEORY.md` now records that UNREACHABLE is a statement about the checker and never about the link, because Python's urllib is refused at the TLS handshake by sites that answer curl without trouble; that every one of the thirty UNREACHABLE rows in the August 2026 sweep was reachable; that web.archive.org was unreachable from the maintainer's machine that day, leaving roughly seventeen Wayback-sourced rows unjudged, the New Jersey contingent-fee scale among them; that americanbar.org gates automated retrieval; and that roughly three quarters of the corpus is either a PDF the checker cannot read or a page it cannot judge, so a sweep finds rot in the quarter it can see and proves nothing about the rest. `MAINTAINING.md` carries the refreshed parser baseline and a step that re-checks every UNREACHABLE row with curl before anyone treats one as a dead link. It also records that a claim-missing verdict is not by itself a genuine change, because the August 2026 sweep produced twenty of them and a hand re-check cleared all twenty.
- Repaired five citations whose closing autolink bracket had landed before the end of the URL. The bare-URL sweep in 0.3.0 stopped at the first open parenthesis in a path, so the bracket fell mid-address and the rest of the URL sat outside the link: a reader clicking one got a truncated address and nothing else. One Michigan Court of Appeals opinion, the Montana disciplinary rules with their adopting order, and the Montana Lawyers' Fund rules. All three fetch as PDFs at the repaired addresses, and a search of every verification file finds no sixth instance.
- Re-checked by hand the twenty rows the sweep reported as no longer carrying their claim, and resolved every one before the release rather than shipping them as plainly confirmed. Most were not defects at all: the checker had tested them against a page the row does not cite, because a source cell reading "same URL as above" sends the resolver to the neighboring row instead of the document the cell names. Three Arizona rows now name that document. Four District of Columbia rows, one North Carolina row and one Utah row moved off an index page onto the rule page carrying the text. `verification_ut.md:57` used the wrong rule-set parameter and returned a rule-not-found page; the corrected URL carries the rule word for word. `verification_ut.md:58` cited a file name rather than a URL and now cites the version marked effective 5/9/2017, which states the 28-day appeal deadline the row asserts, because the undated PDF at the same folder is the superseded one and still says 30 days. `verification_va.md:26` named an intake brochure with no URL; the brochure now has one and carries three of the row's four phone numbers. Two rows are annotated rather than changed: the Topeka page is client-rendered, so no automated checker can see the quote a maintainer read there, and the Massachusetts review-grounds claim is this pack's reading of the rules' incorporation of G.L. c. 251 rather than a sentence in the rules. The five rows citing a Wayback capture now say in their own note that the archive refused every connection during the sweep. Nothing from the twenty is deferred to February.
- Recorded in `THEORY.md` the rows no checker change can reach: a row that names only a host carrying thousands of pages, or reasons by elimination over the rows above it, has no single page behind it and reads as confirmed when it is not. Indiana rows 47 and 48 and Mississippi rows 63 to 65 are the known instances, and the cure is naming the document in the cell.
- Added section 11 of the national baseline, on a lawyer who keeps the file and calls it a retaining lien. The word lien had appeared nowhere in the rules, docs, templates or examples, while a dozen state packs discuss retaining liens. The section explains what a retaining lien is, separates it from a charging lien, and says plainly that there is no general American answer to whether a lawyer may hold a file, because that answer is a matter of state law. `rules/08_file_and_property.md` now names the concept and, where a deadline is running, tells the client to write the prejudice argument immediately using the file-request letter that already exists rather than waiting on a lien question nobody can answer generally.
- Added section 12 of the national baseline, on a fee paid by someone other than the client. A parent, an employer, or another third party who pays does not become the client, is not entitled to the file or to privileged information by paying, and cannot direct the lawyer's judgment. The section refuses to say who is contractually owed an unearned refund in that situation, because that is not a professional-conduct question, and it puts insurance defense out of scope. `rules/04_fee_agreement.md` now tells the reader to check who signed the agreement against who is asking for help.
- Added `scripts/check_sources.py`, a maintenance tool that fetches every URL cited under `references/` and reports whether the page still supports the row's claim. A status code is not a result: it separates pages that returned successfully but no longer carry the claim, pages whose content is rendered client-side, blocked requests, redirects, and PDFs it cannot read, and it reports how many rows it could not parse so a healthy-looking summary cannot hide a barely-checked file. It writes its report outside the repository and makes no changes of its own.
- Added `tests/test_reference_layer.py`. The reference layer is Markdown with no logic to test, so the tests check its shape instead: every pack has a verification file and the reverse, the eight required sections appear in order, the sections that must cite a source do, every verification file carries its Confirmed, Flagged and Gap parts, every row has a claim and a source, and the documented pack count matches the files on disk. It reads the repository directly rather than fixtures, so it fails on the real thing.
- Wrote down the rule that a source is cited by a URL landing on the page holding the claim, and fixed the places that broke it. Utah and Wisconsin rows had recorded only a site's root while their notes described a specific PDF, so the actual document had been lost. Two New Hampshire rows named a page and linked the front page. New York's client protection fund cap now cites the page that states it. The two situations where a front page is the right cite, a claim about the organization itself and a claim whose text is only on the front page, are listed by name in the tests with a reason each, and a further test fails if one of those entries stops matching.
- Moved three rows out of Confirmed that did not belong there. A Mississippi row cited a FindLaw page that turns out to describe Florida's fund, not Mississippi's, and is now flagged as the commercial mirror it is. Another Mississippi row recorded a search that found nothing, which is a gap rather than a confirmation, and joins the gap list entry that already described the same absence. A Kentucky row rested entirely on a third-party case-law database whose own note conceded the majority opinion was never retrieved; Kentucky's verification file had no Flagged section at all, so it has one now, and it holds that row.
- Changed the West Virginia complaint route from two deep links to a description. The disciplinary office hosts its complaint packet and form on Google Drive and a marketing-platform CDN rather than on its own domain, and those URLs will rotate. The pack now names the buttons a reader will see on the single page that is the office's site. The verification row keeps the fetched URLs, because that is what a provenance record is for.
- Wrapped every bare URL and email address in `references/verification_*.md` as an autolink, `<https://...>`, so the verification files pass markdownlint's MD034 rule with no citation changed. Three `www.` hostnames that appear as data rather than as links, in the Idaho and Missouri files, are set in code spans instead, because giving them a scheme would be inventing a URL nobody verified. The homepage exemptions in `tests/test_reference_layer.py` match a row's Source cell exactly, so the three whose cells changed were updated to the bracketed text. `scripts/check_sources.py` is unaffected: its URL pattern stops at the closing bracket, so it fetches the same addresses as before.
- Declared the project complete in scope and wrote down how it is kept that way. `THEORY.md` holds what a maintainer needs to know before changing anything: the citation invariants, the sites that return success without the text, the decisions that look wrong on purpose, and the two places the model is thin. `MAINTAINING.md` holds the twice-yearly source sweep as a bounded procedure, with the parser baseline to check before trusting a report and the rule that a sweep fixes or cuts and never widens scope. `FUTURE_FEATURES.md` and `BUILD_PLAN.md` no longer carry a queued item; per-county program detail was already rejected and the build plan now says so instead of listing it as next. `scripts/README.md` states the Python version the scripts are tested on. `SECURITY.md` no longer says the kit ships no network code and one script: it ships three optional scripts, and the source checker is network code that a maintainer runs by hand. The README sentence that said the only script checks the tracker's format is corrected the same way.
- Five verification files (Hawaii, Maine, Mississippi, Montana, and the national baseline) had a blank line inside a citation table, so GitHub rendered the 36 rows below it as plain paragraphs and `scripts/check_sources.py` either skipped them or read the wrong column as the claim. The blank lines are removed, the checker now reports a table row with no header above it as unparseable instead of guessing, and a test checks the table shape of every verification file.

## 2026-08-21 (0.3.0)

Shipped. Tagged and pushed on 2026-08-21.

- Added `examples/hourly_retainer_not_a_cap.md`, the first worked example where the client turns out to be wrong. Every other example ends with a claim the reader can act on. This one shows what the session says when the record does not support the complaint, and what the client can still legitimately ask for without a demand letter.
- Added `docs/DECISION_TREE_ONE_PAGE.md`, a printable one-sheet condensation of the decision tree. It adds no facts. Every branch and every condition from the full tree survives.
- Added `scripts/export_chronology.py` and its tests. It turns a filled-in tracker into a date-sorted plain-text chronology for a bar complaint or fee arbitration submission. It refuses to export a tracker the validator would reject, and rows with an unparseable date are reported rather than silently dropped.
- Added `docs/PROVABLE_DELIVERY.md`, on choosing a delivery method that lets you prove a letter arrived. It covers proving delivery, not legal service of process, and says so. Two facts that only commercial sources would confirm, how long USPS keeps a delivery record and how long an uncollected certified letter is held, were cut rather than stated.
- Added contingent-fee dispute guidance to section 4 of the national baseline. Model Rule 1.5(a) reasonableness applies to a contingent fee with no carve-out, and a contingent dispute is about the closing statement arithmetic rather than hours worked. No percentage cap or dollar figure is stated; those belong to the state packs.
- Added section 10 of the national baseline, for clients whose lawyer died, was suspended or disbarred, or left practice. The duty to return the file and refund unearned money is not conditioned on why the representation ended. The section routes the reader to their state pack's disciplinary authority to find who now holds the file.
- Sourced the New Jersey contingent-fee sliding scale to the Judiciary's own archived site rather than the two commercial mirrors it had rested on. The current njcourts.gov does not serve the rule text to automated retrieval, so the confirmed source is a 2015 capture of the Judiciary's predecessor domain. Both the pack and the row now say plainly that the kit confirmed the 2015 wording and not today's, and tell the reader to confirm the percentages with the court or a lawyer.
- Added `docs/FIND_YOUR_LOCAL_PROGRAM.md`, a search procedure and confirmation checklist for readers whose state runs fee arbitration through local committees. It names no state, county, program, deadline, or dollar figure, so it makes no claim needing a verification row. It also tells the model working with the reader that "I could not confirm a local program in your county" is a correct answer.
- Retired per-county fee dispute compilation from the backlog to the explicitly rejected list. The kit will not try to cover every county in the country. Local programs change faster than a kit like this could track, and a stale committee name reads exactly like a current one. The search document above is the answer instead, and a contributor who confirms a local program against a primary source can still add it to a state pack.

## 2026-08-21 (0.2.0, all fifty states and the District of Columbia)

Shipped. Merged to main on 2026-08-21.

- Generalized the kit from Tennessee to every state. It now ships a national baseline built on the ABA Model Rules plus one pack per state, each following the same eight sections, with its own verification file.
- Added `references/national_baseline.md` and `references/state_template.md`, and turned `references/VERIFICATION.md` into the citation standard plus an index of every per-state verification file.
- Rewrote the forum selection rule, the decision tree and the templates to branch on the reader's state rather than assuming Tennessee's forums.
- Folded the three Tennessee reference files into `references/state_tn.md` and removed them. Tennessee readers lose nothing; the same claims are in the pack, with the same sourcing.
- Added a District of Columbia pack. DC is not a state, but it has its own bar, its own disciplinary system, its own client protection fund, and a fee arbitration rule that binds the lawyer once a qualifying client files, so it needed a pack of its own rather than being read off a neighbor.
- Added two worked examples for the routing branches Tennessee does not show: Maine, where the client can require the lawyer into fee arbitration, and Alabama, where no fee arbitration program exists at all and a demand letter and small claims are the only path.
- Added the informal conciliation step to the Virginia pack. The Virginia State Bar fee dispute program opens with a discretionary conciliation attempt by the committee chair before mediation or arbitration. It is described only in the program manual, not on the program page, which is why it was missed.
- Repaired dead source links in eight state packs (Montana, Indiana, North Carolina, South Dakota, Arkansas, New Mexico, Utah, Michigan). Every claim was re-read at its current official location. No dollar figure, deadline, or program name changed. South Dakota moved off a Wayback snapshot onto a live official source, and Indiana now states plainly that its fee dispute page is gone rather than suggesting a reader might reach it.
- Changed the Oregon client protection fund contact from a named bar staff member, with a direct email and phone, to the bar main line with the fund named. A public kit should route a reader to a program, not to one person, and a named contact goes stale when that person changes roles.
- Corrected the workflow stage count in `llm/WORKFLOW.md`, which said seven stages while defining eight. Adding the state-establishing Stage 0 created the off-by-one.

### Corrections to claims that were public in 0.1.0

- Removed the claim that a consumer assistance program will follow up with a lawyer who has not responded to correspondence within ten days. That was a Tennessee program with a Tennessee deadline, stated in `rules/07_communication.md` and `rules/08_file_and_property.md` as though it applied to any reader. Most states run no such program, and those that do do not all use ten days. The rules now point at whatever intake track section 4 of the reader's own state pack names, and say not to promise a response time the pack does not state.
- Corrected `rules/04_fee_agreement.md`, which asserted a tenth reasonableness factor under Rule 1.5(a). Tennessee has ten factors. The ABA model has eight, and most states follow the model. The rule now tells the reader to check section 2 of their own state pack before naming a factor by number.
- Replaced the abbreviation RPC throughout the templates and rules. RPC is Tennessee's own name for its rules. Templates now refer to a rule by number and say it is a rule of professional conduct in the reader's state.
- Renamed the tracker's `rpc_touchpoint` column to `rule_touchpoint`, in the column dictionary, both TOML schemas, the validator, the CSV template and the tests.

## 2026-08-10 (reference layer accuracy follow-up)

- Confirmed the Tennessee Rules of Professional Conduct content against a current, dated source: the Tennessee Bar Association's 2023 Edition, amendments through November 30, 2022, replacing the redline document the rule text was originally checked against.
- Corrected RPC 1.16(d) in `references/tennessee-professional-conduct.md`: the rule lists six steps to protect a client's interests on discharge or withdrawal, not five. Surrendering papers and property the client is entitled to and surrendering other work product are separate steps.
- Added `references/tennessee-deadlines.md`, sourcing five deadlines: no time bar on a Board disciplinary complaint, the Client Protection Fund's 60-day complaint gate and its separate 3-year and 5-year loss-based limits, the six-year contract limitation, the one-year legal malpractice limitation with its five-year repose, and the confirmed-versus-unconfirmed filing-window status of the seven local fee dispute programs.
- Updated `references/VERIFICATION.md` with the new sourcing and two caveats: the statute text came from a commercial code mirror rather than the official Tennessee Code, and tncourts.gov remains unreachable to automated retrieval.
