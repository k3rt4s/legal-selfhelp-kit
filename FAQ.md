# FAQ

**Is this legal advice?**

No. It is a way to organize your own facts and draft your own letters. It does not create an attorney-client relationship with anyone.

**Will this get my lawyer disbarred?**

Almost certainly not, and that is the wrong goal to hold. Most complaints do not result in public discipline, because most describe disappointing service rather than a rules violation. If your goal is money back, discipline is not the route.

**Will I get my money back?**

Unknown. This kit does not predict outcomes. What it does is make sure you ask the right forum, in writing, with the documents that support you.

**Do I need a lawyer to use this?**

No. But there are situations where you should get one anyway: money missing from a trust account, a missed court deadline, an active case, a firm suing you, or a malpractice claim.

**Why does it not quote the rules?**

Because the official publication point for the Tennessee Rules of Professional Conduct did not return readable rule text to automated retrieval when this kit was built, so the rule text here comes from a secondary comparison document. [references/VERIFICATION.md](references/VERIFICATION.md) explains exactly what that means. Sending a quotation you have not verified hands the other side an easy dismissal, so the templates reference rules by number instead.

**Does it work outside Tennessee?**

The approach does. The specifics do not. The forums, the General Sessions limits, the Consumer Assistance Program, and the client protection fund are all Tennessee.

**Which model should I use?**

Any model that accepts file uploads and holds a long context. If yours has a small window, use [llm/QUICKSTART.md](llm/QUICKSTART.md).

**Do I have to run any code?**

No. The one script is optional and only checks whether your tracker CSV is formatted correctly.

**Can I use this for a dispute with someone other than my lawyer?**

No. It is built around the rules that govern lawyers and the forums that hear complaints about them.

**Who made this?**

Jon Bowker. It is published free under MIT because the process of disputing a legal bill should not itself require hiring someone.
