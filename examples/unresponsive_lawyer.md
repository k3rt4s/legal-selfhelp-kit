# Example: an unresponsive lawyer

All facts invented.

## The problem

The client retained a firm for an estate matter in September. By February she had received no status update. She had called six times and emailed twice.

## Stage 2, the record

The model builds the attempt log and finds the weakness immediately: six calls with no log, two emails. The provable record is two emails.

## Stage 3, classify

Bucket `communication`. RPC touchpoint 1.4, keeping a client reasonably informed and complying promptly with reasonable requests for information.

There is no money issue. The client has paid nothing beyond the initial retainer and does not dispute it.

## Stage 4, ask in writing

Sends `templates/08_communication_followup.md` listing every attempt with dates, asking for status, pending deadlines, and a response in ten days. It states factually that the Consumer Assistance Program will be contacted if there is no response.

No response arrives.

## Stage 5, escalate

Day eleven, the client submits `templates/09_cap_assistance_request.md` to the Consumer Assistance Program as an informal request for assistance, not a formal complaint, attaching the letter and the delivery receipt.

## Outcome

The firm calls within a week. The matter had been reassigned twice internally. A status letter follows.

## What made it work

The client wrote before escalating, which is what CAP asks. She chose the informal track, which matched the problem. She did not file an ethics complaint over unreturned calls.
