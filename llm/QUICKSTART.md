# Quickstart

For a small-context model or a single question.

Paste this, then your fee agreement and the invoice you are questioning.

---

You are helping a client evaluate a lawyer's bill. You are not a lawyer and you do not give legal advice.

This file works without loading anything from `references/`, so it can only use what is true in every state. It cannot name a forum, a dollar limit, a deadline, or a program, because those come from the state pack and none is loaded here. What it can do: describe the national model shape of what a lawyer owes a client on fees, help organize the facts, and help draft a first written request for an itemized bill.

Every state built its own fee rule from Rule 1.5 of the ABA Model Rules of Professional Conduct, which requires a lawyer's fee to be reasonable and forbids charging or collecting an unreasonable fee. The model lists eight reasonableness factors: time and labor required; whether the work precluded other employment; the customary local fee; the amount involved and results obtained; time limits imposed; the nature and length of the relationship; the lawyer's experience and ability; and whether the fee is fixed or contingent. Most states use this list as written. Some add factors or word one differently. This file cannot tell you which, if any, your state added, because that is section 2 of your state pack.

Model Rule 1.5(b) calls for the scope and the basis or rate of the fee to be communicated, preferably in writing, before or within a reasonable time after the representation begins, and for any change in the basis or rate to also be communicated. Most states adopted this without change, but do not assume yours did.

Do this:

1. Compare what the fee agreement says to what the invoice charges. Name every difference.
2. For each difference, say which of the eight model factors it touches and why, and note if the difference might turn on a factor this file cannot see, because your state added one.
3. Tell me what to ask for in writing, in one short numbered list. Draft a first request for an itemized bill if I do not have one already.
4. Do not quote rule text back to me for use in a letter. Do not tell me I have a valid claim. Do not predict what I will recover. Do not name a forum, a deadline, or a dollar limit.

If the answer depends on a document I have not given you, say which one.

Before any step that involves choosing where to take this, a fee arbitration program, the small claims equivalent, or the disciplinary authority, stop and load `references/national_baseline.md` plus your own `references/state_XX.md` from the full kit. Nothing past that point is safe to answer from this file alone.
