## Gap list

1. EAP Op. 96-35, "Attorney lien upon termination" (per the EAP Subject Index topic listing),
   is the opinion most directly on point for whether a Rhode Island lawyer may withhold a
   client's file over unpaid fees, and is cited by name inside EAP Op. 2026-04 and EAP Op.
   2026-08 on a related but narrower point (no charge for copying; third-party consent). Its
   own full text was located, after the current-opinions URL pattern
   (https://www.courts.ri.gov/Opinions/EAP-YYYY-NN.pdf) 404'd, via an older undocumented path
   (courts.ri.gov/AttorneyResources/ethicsadvisorypanel/Opinions/) found through the Wayback
   Machine, and read in full from rendered page images since the scanned PDF has no text
   layer. It treats the statutory lien and the file-surrender duty as separate obligations and
   does not authorize refusing to hand over a file for unpaid fees. The remaining gap is
   narrower: no EAP opinion located addresses the exact fact pattern of a lawyer outright
   refusing to return a file until paid, so section 2 reports what the sources actually read
   establish rather than asserting an absolute rule either way.
2. The original date Rhode Island's Supreme Court first adopted the current Model
   Rules-numbering-based version of Article V (as opposed to the "Revised October 2023"
   edition date, which is a republication date, not an original adoption date) was not stated
   in the rules text itself and was not independently researched further. Left out of section 1
   rather than guessed.
3. Whether Rhode Island case law recognizes a rule violation as admissible evidence of the
   standard of care in an actual malpractice case, and how courts have applied that in
   practice, was not researched. Article V's Scope comment states the possibility in the
   abstract; confirming how Rhode Island courts have used it would require a case-law search,
   which is outside this pack's sourcing method (official rule and statute text, not case law)
   and outside this kit's scope (malpractice strategy). Left out of section 8 rather than
   asserted.
4. Whether Rhode Island has a statutory contingent-fee percentage cap or sliding scale outside
   Article V, for example in a specific practice area, was checked only within Rule 1.5 itself,
   where none was found. A broader search of the General Laws outside Titles 8, 9, and 10 for
   such a cap was not performed. Left out of section 2 rather than asserted as confirmed
   absent.
5. Whether any Rhode Island rule or Bar publication states a formal notice requirement for a
   lawyer to tell a client about the right to fee arbitration before suing for fees was not
   found in the Fee Arbitration Committee Rules or on the Bar's fee arbitration page. Stated in
   the pack as "not found" rather than affirmatively "does not exist," since the rules were not
   exhaustively searched beyond the fourteen numbered Rules read.
6. Whether Rhode Island's small claims chapter or any District Court rule outside Chapter 16
   separately restricts or authorizes attorney appearance was checked only within G.L. Chapter
   10-16 itself, where nothing on the subject beyond the narrow corporate-representative
   provision in section 10-16-3.1 was found. The District Court's own Rules of Civil Procedure
   or local small-claims administrative rules, if any exist outside Chapter 16, were not
   separately searched. Left out of section 6 as "not addressed" rather than asserted either
   way.
7. Article4-2.pdf (Article IV(2)) and Article6.pdf (Article VI) of the Supreme Court Rules were
   fetched during this research pass but not closely analyzed, since neither appeared on point
   for the eight required sections once the more directly relevant sources (Article V, the
   Disciplinary Board rules, and the EAP materials) were located. If a future revision of this
   pack needs bar admission or a different disciplinary-procedure detail, these two files are a
   starting point.
