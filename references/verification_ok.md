## Gap list

1. Discovery rule for legal malpractice. This pack looked for an Oklahoma Supreme Court
   decision establishing when the limitations clock starts for a legal malpractice claim,
   specifically whether Oklahoma applies a discovery rule the way it does by statute for
   medical malpractice. A case that initially looked on point (Reynolds v. Porter, 1988 OK
   88) turned out on closer reading to be a medical malpractice case, not a legal
   malpractice case, and was excluded. No on-point, directly fetchable legal malpractice
   discovery-rule case was found. The pack does not state a discovery rule for legal
   malpractice and flags this as unresolved.
2. Statute of repose for legal malpractice. A search was run for any Oklahoma statute of
   repose specific to claims against attorneys. None was found. The pack does not state a
   repose period.
3. Tort-versus-contract classification of a legal malpractice claim. This pack infers that a
   legal malpractice claim would fall under the general 2-year "injury to rights not
   arising on contract" period in 12 O.S. section 95(A)(3), based on the statute's own
   language, but no Oklahoma case or secondary authority specifically classifying legal
   malpractice claims for limitations purposes was independently fetched and confirmed. The
   pack states this as the period "most likely to govern," not as a settled
   classification. This reviewer re-checked this hedge and confirmed it still reads as
   unsettled, not as a resolved rule.
4. Grievance filing deadline. The Rules Governing Disciplinary Proceedings PDF (5 O.S., Ch.
   1, App. 1-A, at <https://www.okbar.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/RulesGoverningDisciplinary.pdf>)
   was fetched and searched with targeted keyword passes for any filing deadline on a
   client grievance. None was found in those passes. This is recorded as an absence finding
   from a targeted search, not a claim that a full, provision-by-provision read of the
   106-page document was completed. If a deadline matters to a reader's situation, it
   should be confirmed directly with the Office of the General Counsel.
5. OBA Ethics Opinion No. 312, on mandatory arbitration clauses in fee agreements. This
   opinion was identified only through a WebSearch summary, which this pack's sourcing rule
   does not treat as usable on its own. It was not independently fetched and read by either
   reviewer. The pack references the general possibility of an arbitration clause in a fee
   agreement without describing the opinion's specific content or citing it by number.
6. Oklahoma Scope section and use of a rule violation as evidence of a standard of care. The
   national baseline describes an ABA Model Rules comment sentence saying a rule violation
   may be used as evidence of a breach of the standard of conduct in an otherwise recognized
   claim. Oklahoma's Scope section, paragraphs 19 through 22, was fetched and read in full,
   and no equivalent sentence was found in that text. This is recorded as an absence finding
   from the fetched text, not as confirmation that Oklahoma has affirmatively rejected the
   idea elsewhere in its case law, which was not searched.
7. Completeness of the local county fee arbitration program list. Only the Tulsa County Bar
   Association's program was confirmed in enough detail to describe with confidence, and the
   Oklahoma County Bar Association's committee was found but not confirmed to function as
   arbitration. No attempt was made to survey Oklahoma's other counties. The pack states
   plainly that its county-level list is incomplete.
8. Whether OBA Ethics Opinion 295 has been reaffirmed, distinguished, or relied on in any
   reported Oklahoma Supreme Court disciplinary decision since the 2008 rules took effect.
   This reviewer checked the OBA's own topical index for a newer opinion on the same subject
   and found none, and checked that current Rule 1.16(d)/Comment 9 and Rule 1.15(d) do not
   conflict with the opinion's outcome, but did not search Oklahoma Supreme Court
   disciplinary opinions themselves for a citation to Opinion 295. If a reader's situation
   turns on how settled this is, that search should be done before relying on it.
9. Whether "hiring a lawyer is optional" in Oklahoma small claims court traces to a specific
   statute. No provision of the Small Claims Procedure Act was found that says this in so
   many words; the pack now states this as a description of the Act's informal design (12
   O.S. section 1761, "informal ... sole object of dispensing speedy justice") rather than
   as a specific statutory permission, and no separate citation is offered for it.
