## Gap list

1. WSBA Fee Arbitration Program's operational rules, mandatory-or-voluntary status, binding
   effect, right to trial afterward, dollar ceiling or floor, filing deadline, filing fee, and
   any pre-suit notice requirement, were not found. Search covered the WSBA's sitemap (over
   6,700 URLs, no current match), direct guesses at plausible URLs (all returned 404), the
   WSBA's own site search (returns no static results, appears to require client-side
   rendering), Google, Bing, and DuckDuckGo scraped directly (each blocked, redirected, or
   returned no wsba.org-specific results), and the Wayback Machine's CDX index of wsba.org
   (only 2000-2003 snapshots of a fee-arbitration page, too old to reflect current rules). The
   program's bare existence and WSBA administration are confirmed; its rules are not.
2. Local county or city bar association fee dispute committees, for example any King County
   Bar Association program, were not surveyed. Only the statewide WSBA-administered picture
   was researched given the scope of this pack.
3. An aggregate, per-lawyer lifetime cap on Client Protection Fund payouts across multiple
   claims was not found. Neither the WSBA's Application Procedures document nor the court's
   Procedural Regulations states a separate multi-claim ceiling per lawyer, under either the
   $150,000 or the $250,000 per-application figure.
4. A statute of repose for legal malpractice, an outer time limit running regardless of
   discovery, was not found. The search covered the RCW 4.16 series sections fetched above and
   one Court of Appeals opinion; no repose provision was located in that material.
5. Whether a Rules of Professional Conduct violation is admissible as evidence of the standard
   of care in a Washington legal malpractice case was not researched. The kit does not cover
   malpractice strategy.
6. The Washington Consumer Protection Act (RCW 19.86) and whether it reaches attorney billing
   practices was not researched and is not covered in the pack.
7. Any typical dollar recovery in a fee dispute, mediation, or small claims case was not
   sought; no verified dataset exists for this and none was claimed.
8. The Court of Appeals opinion used for the malpractice accrual point mentions a
   continuous-representation tolling argument, but the court's ruling on it was not confirmed
   with certainty from the extracted text, so continuous-representation tolling is not stated
   as Washington law anywhere in the pack.
9. The dollar amount of the RCW 7.75.035 surcharge added to the small claims filing fee was
   not looked up; only its existence and statutory basis, confirmed in RCW 12.40.020's own
   text, are stated in the pack.
10. Whether any Washington rule specifically addresses attorney fee disputes brought in small
    claims court was searched for and not found; the pack states this as a confirmed absence
    within the material reviewed, not as an exhaustive negative.

## How to extend this file

Add a row to the confirmed table for any new claim, with its source URL and retrieval date,
before adding the claim to state_wa.md. If the best available source is not primary or
official, add the claim to the flagged table with a reason instead. If no adequate source can
be found, add the item to the gap list and leave it out of the pack.
