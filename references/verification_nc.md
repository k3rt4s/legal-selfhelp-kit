## Gap list

1. Whether Comment 7's "divorce, alimony, or support" contingent-fee restriction extends to a
   property settlement, the way the Model Rule's own text does, was not confirmed. The comment
   text itself names only divorce, alimony, and support; no broader statement or a case
   construing it was located. The pack states the comment's own terms and does not assume the
   broader Model Rule scope applies.
2. Whether Client Security Fund awards are ordinarily paid in full up to the $100,000 cap, or
   reduced by a discretionary percentage when total claims against one attorney or in one year
   are heavy relative to the Fund's assets, was not clearly confirmed. 27 NCAC 1D .1418(e) lists
   the total losses caused by one attorney and the Fund's available assets among the factors the
   board weighs, which supports that a reduction is possible, but no statement of current or
   typical practice was located. Search stopped at the .1418 rule text as fetched.
3. No deadline running from the date of a loss, or from its discovery, was located anywhere in
   the Client Security Fund rules read for this pack (.1401, .1416, .1417, .1418). Search stopped
   at those sections as fetched; such a deadline may exist in a Client Security Fund rule section
   not reached during this research (for example .1402 through .1415 were not individually
   fetched). The separate question of whether a grievance or fee dispute petition must be filed
   first is resolved and no longer a gap: see the confirmed table's row on the Fund's own public
   page, which describes a grievance as a recommended first step and exhaustion of remedies as
   requiring, at minimum, a fee dispute petition where the loss involves a fee.
4. Whether a North Carolina legal malpractice claim sounds in tort, in contract, or is available
   either way at the client's election was not confirmed against a case holding read directly
   for this pack. G.S. 1-15(c)'s own text does not resolve this characterization question, and
   no North Carolina appellate opinion on the point was independently fetched and read. The pack
   states the statute's own accrual and discovery-rule language without asserting a
   characterization the statute does not itself state.
5. Whether a Rules of Professional Conduct violation is admissible as evidence of the standard
   of care in a North Carolina legal malpractice case was not researched. This kit does not
   cover malpractice strategy, so research stopped at the limitation periods needed to tell a
   reader a clock exists.
6. A single located secondary source described a North Carolina Court of Appeals decision
   addressing the defensive use of a Rule 1.8(a) violation. That source was a law firm blog, and
   per this kit's sourcing rules a law firm blog cannot confirm a claim on its own. No primary
   opinion was independently located and read, so this claim was cut entirely rather than
   included with a flag. Search stopped at the blog post.
7. G.S. 97-90's Industrial Commission fee-approval process for workers' compensation matters,
   including its appeal path to the full Commission and then to superior court, was read in
   full, but this pack treats the entire subject as out of scope, because it is a specialized
   fee-approval regime for one practice area rather than a general reasonableness or percentage
   rule of the kind Section 2 covers, and because this kit does not cover specialized-forum fee
   regulation. It is noted in Section 2 only to explain why a commonly cited "25%" cap was not
   used, not as an affirmative claim about the workers' compensation fee-approval process.
8. No North Carolina case law was located showing that a court has actually dismissed, stayed,
   or otherwise enforced against a lawyer's fee suit the 30-day waiting period and the
   claim-preservation-only exception described in 27 NCAC 1D .0707(a). The pack describes what
   the State Bar's own procedural rule requires of the lawyer, sourced directly to that rule's
   text, but does not claim this is a confirmed civil-procedure defense a court is shown to
   apply. Search stopped after reading Rule 1.5, its Comments, and the full .0700-series rule
   text; no case law search beyond that was performed.
