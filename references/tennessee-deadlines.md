# Tennessee filing deadlines for a fee or conduct dispute with your own lawyer

Filing deadlines and limitation periods for a Board complaint, a Client Protection Fund claim, a contract or malpractice suit, and local fee dispute programs, confirmed against Tennessee statutes, case law, and program materials where available.

Read [VERIFICATION.md](VERIFICATION.md) for the source and retrieval date behind each deadline below.

## Board of Professional Responsibility disciplinary complaint

There is no deadline for filing a complaint with the Board. The Tennessee Supreme Court has held that laches, the defense that a claim was brought too late, does not apply to attorney disciplinary proceedings at all. In *Long v. Board of Professional Responsibility*, Tenn. 2014, the Court rejected an attorney's argument that discipline should be barred because the underlying conduct was years old, holding that laches cannot be imputed to the Board because its purpose is protecting the public, not deciding a private claim.

Practical read: if you are wondering whether it is too late to complain about something that happened years ago, it is not. The complaint itself has no clock.

## Tennessee Lawyers' Fund for Client Protection

The Fund has two separate deadlines, and missing either one can end a claim before it is considered on the merits.

1. The 60-day gate: you must have a written complaint on file with the Board. If the Board notifies the Fund that no complaint is on file, you have 60 days from that notice to file one, or the Fund claim is dismissed.
2. The substantive deadline, running from the loss itself: for a loss on or after October 1, 2013, a claim must be filed within 3 years of the loss or of when you reasonably should have discovered it, and the Fund will not consider a claim more than 5 years after the loss no matter when it was discovered. A different, older rule applies to losses before October 1, 2013.

Practical read: file the Board complaint promptly, then file with the Fund. Waiting to see how the Board complaint turns out before starting the Fund claim is a real risk to the 3-year and 5-year clocks.

## Getting money back through a contract claim

A claim to recover money for a lawyer's breach of the fee agreement, such as failing to refund an unearned fee, is a contract claim. Tennessee's statute of limitations for a contract claim not otherwise covered by a more specific statute is six years from the breach, at Tenn. Code Ann. § 28-3-109(a)(3).

The clock generally starts running at the breach itself, for example when the lawyer failed to refund money owed. A narrow exception can push the start date later for a breach that is genuinely undiscoverable, but Tennessee courts have treated that exception as limited, so do not count on it to extend the six years.

## Legal malpractice

Malpractice is a claim that the lawyer's own negligence caused you harm, and it needs its own counsel rather than self-help. Two deadlines apply at the same time, at Tenn. Code Ann. § 28-3-104(c):

1. One year from when you discovered, or reasonably should have discovered, the injury and that the lawyer's negligence caused it.
2. An outer limit of five years from the date of the lawyer's act or omission, regardless of when you discovered it. That five-year limit only gives way if the lawyer fraudulently concealed the wrongdoing, in which case you get one year from discovering the concealment.

Practical read: even a claim you discovered promptly can already be dead if the underlying act happened more than five years ago. Do not sit on a possible malpractice question while you pursue a fee dispute through other channels.

## Local fee dispute programs

No filing deadline was found for the Knoxville, Memphis, or Chattanooga fee dispute programs, confirmed directly against each program's own materials.

For Bristol, Kingsport, Montgomery County, and Washington County, no deadline appears in the Board's own consolidated description of these programs, but each program's own rules could not be independently located and read. If you plan to use one of these four, ask the local bar association directly whether any filing window applies before you assume there is none.
