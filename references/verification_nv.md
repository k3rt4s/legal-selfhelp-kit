# Verification: Nevada (state_nv.md)

This is an independent verification pass, done without trusting the prior version of
this file. Every row below reflects a source this reviewer fetched directly. Retrieved
2026-08-20 unless noted. RPC and SCR text came from
<https://www.leg.state.nv.us/CourtRules/RPC.html> and
<https://www.leg.state.nv.us/CourtRules/SCR.html>, fetched with a Chrome user agent and
a Google referer, saved locally, and grepped for the specific rule heading and operative
text before being treated as confirmed. NRS text came the same way from
leg.state.nv.us/NRS. State Bar of Nevada (nvbar.org) pages and PDFs were fetched
directly; PDF text was extracted with pypdf.

## Gap list

1. Looked for the specific published date of the most recent amendment to Rule 1.5,
   Rule 1.15, and Rule 1.16 individually, beyond the rule set's general "[Added;
   effective May 1, 2006]" annotation. Rule 1.5 carries a later amendment annotation
   (4/19/21) visible in the RPC's own table of rule dates; Rule 1.15 and Rule 1.16 show
   only the original 2006 date in that table. Not independently confirmed further; the
   pack does not state a specific later amendment date for any of these rules.
2. Looked for whether NRS 597.860 and NRS 597.870, the two named exceptions to Nevada's
   general bar on small-claims attorney's fees (NRS 73.040), have any bearing on a fee
   or billing dispute with your own lawyer. Not read in this session; out of scope
   because this kit's own reading of the exception's caption suggested it is unrelated
   to attorney-client fee disputes, but that was not independently confirmed by reading
   the statute text.
3. Looked for whether Nevada's district court appeal from a justice court small claims
   judgment is a trial de novo or a record-based review. Read the current Justice Court
   Rules of Civil Procedure, Rules 72, 72A, and 72B (amended effective October 29,
   2024), in full, and searched NRS Chapter 73 and NRS Chapter 4 for an explicit
   statement. None was found. Rule 72(f) describes the justice court clerk forwarding
   docket entries and other papers to the district court, consistent with either a
   record-based review or a trial de novo using the forwarded record as a starting
   point, and does not resolve the question either way. The pack states this as an open
   question rather than picking a side.
4. Looked for whether a corporate party must be represented by a lawyer in a Nevada
   justice court small claims matter, separate from the general "a party may appear
   without a lawyer" default. No specific corporate-representation rule was found in NRS
   Chapter 73 or the current Justice Court Rules of Civil Procedure. The pack does not
   state a corporate-representation rule.
5. Looked for a Nevada-specific statute of repose applying to legal malpractice claims,
   separate from the discovery-rule and damage-accrual periods in NRS 11.207. NRS
   Chapter 11 was read in full and no malpractice-specific repose provision, distinct
   from the ordinary limitation periods already stated, was found. This is omitted from
   the pack.
6. Looked for whether NRS 11.207's "whether based on a breach of duty or contract"
   language has been construed by a Nevada appellate decision to fully displace the
   ordinary contract-limitation periods in NRS 11.190 for a malpractice-flavored dispute
   over legal fees. This kit did not fetch or read any case law within the scope of this
   session, only the statute's own text. The pack states the statute's plain wording and
   flags that this is not confirmed against case law.
7. Looked for an aggregate, fund-wide, or per-lawyer annual cap on Clients' Security
   Fund payouts, separate from the $50,000 per-applicant figure. The Rules of Procedure
   PDF was read in full; the only cap language found is the per-applicant figure and the
   Committee's hardship-adjustment discretion, plus a pro-rating provision if the Fund
   lacks money for all approved claims in a period. No separate aggregate or annual cap
   was found. The pack states only the per-applicant figure.
8. Did not attempt to confirm whether the Fee Dispute Committee's or Office of Bar
   Counsel's current published contact information (address, phone, email) matches the
   nvbar.org pages fetched this session, since none of that contact detail is stated in
   the pack; the pack points readers to the URLs directly instead.
9. Did not independently verify the current name of any Bar Counsel, Committee Chair, or
   Client Protection Coordinator, and deliberately did not use any such name in the pack,
   per this kit's rule against naming a current officeholder. Only office titles are
   used.
10. Did not check whether Nevada's fee arbitration, discipline, or Clients' Security
    Fund materials have a Spanish-language or other translated version, or whether
    individual Nevada justice courts publish local small claims procedures or forms
    beyond the statewide statute and Justice Court Rules of Civil Procedure cited in
    section 6.
11. Did not read Nevada appellate case law construing NRS 18.015's paragraph (b) file
    lien, so this kit cannot say whether any published decision has narrowed or
    qualified the statute's plain text (for example, on what counts as "properly left
    in the possession of the attorney," or on the outer edge of the subsection 6
    adjudication remedy). The pack states the statute's plain wording only.
