## Gap list

1. Rule 1.16 (Declining or Terminating Representation) could not be read this session. It has
   apparently never been archived by the Wayback Machine under any URL slug tried
   (rule-116-declining-or-terminating-representation and similar variants all returned no
   snapshot, confirmed by a broad CDX prefix sweep of the whole RPC URL tree, not just one
   guessed URL), and live retrieval of courts.nh.gov was blocked throughout. The pack's
   file-return discussion now rests on the Rule 1.15 comment's own citation of Rule 1.16(d), on
   NHBA Ethics Committee Opinion #2015-16/05 (read directly, see the Confirmed table), and on
   *Averill v. Cox* (case existence confirmed via CourtListener, holding as generally
   characterized, flagged table). None of these substitutes is Rule 1.16's own text. Confirm
   Rule 1.16 directly before relying on any specific list of steps it requires.
2. New Hampshire Supreme Court Rule 55, the rule establishing the Public Protection Fund, could
   not be read this session. It returned no Wayback Machine snapshot under either URL path tried
   (the RPC path and the Supreme Court Rules path where Rule 55 actually lives), confirmed by a
   broad CDX prefix sweep, and live retrieval of courts.nh.gov was blocked. A 1998 New Hampshire
   Supreme Court opinion quoting Rule 55(3) and (4) verbatim, from when the rule was adopted, was
   found and is now in the pack: a $150,000 per-lawyer cap and a $1,000,000 annual aggregate cap.
   These are 1998 figures from a case construing the rule, not the current rule text; whether
   either cap has since been amended is unconfirmed. The Fund's own regulations (read in full)
   describe eligibility, exclusions, and procedure but do not themselves state a dollar limit.
3. The 1999 NHBA article on client file retention, and ABA Informal Opinion 1384, were not
   independently read; see the flagged table.
4. Whether the NHBA Dispute Resolution Committee has ever had, or currently has, an unstated
   internal dollar range it typically handles was not researched beyond the committee's own
   published procedures, which state no floor or ceiling. Ask the DRC directly before assuming a
   large claim is out of scope.
5. Whether a lawyer must notify a client of the right to use the DRC before suing the client for
   fees was searched for in the DRC's own procedures and not found; this pack states the absence
   as a finding, not as a confirmed "no such rule exists anywhere in New Hampshire law," since a
   requirement could in principle exist in a different rule not reviewed this session.
6. Whether legal malpractice in New Hampshire is formally classified as sounding in tort,
   contract, or either at the plaintiff's election was not resolved by locating a case or
   secondary source saying so directly. The pack instead reports the practical effect confirmed
   by statute: RSA 508:4 applies the same 3-year, same-discovery-rule period regardless of how
   the claim is framed, which was read directly from the statute. No case law on the
   tort-versus-contract classification question itself was read.
7. Whether the Complaint Screening Committee's own internal review criteria, beyond what the FAQ
   describes ("no reasonable basis... by clear and convincing evidence"), amount to a formally
   named "informal track" distinct from a formal complaint was not confirmed beyond the FAQ and
   About Us pages read. The pack states that no such named split was found in what was read, not
   that no split exists anywhere in Rule 37 or Rule 37A's full text.
8. The Rule 49 $250 Supreme Court filing fee for a small claims appeal was checked specifically
   for a surcharge disclaimer. The Circuit Court filing fee schedule (used for the original small
   claims filing) carries a general note that a "total fee" may include unnamed statutory
   surcharges. The Rule 49 Supreme Court schedule itself, read in full, carries no such
   disclaimer anywhere in its text; the word "surcharge" does not appear in it at all. Read as
   written, $250 is the complete Supreme Court appeal fee, on top of which only the recording and
   transcription cost under RSA 503:10 applies. This narrows what was previously an open gap; it
   does not rule out an unannounced future fee change, which no document read this session could
   confirm either way.
9. No dollar figure for what any particular remedy typically recovers. There is no verified
   dataset behind such a number, and none was sought.
10. Current officeholder names for the ADO, PCC, Hearings Committee, or Public Protection Fund
    Committee were deliberately not recorded or used, consistent with this kit's sourcing rules.

## How to extend this file

Any new rule, deadline, dollar amount, phone number, or program name added to
[state_nh.md](state_nh.md) gets a row in one of the tables above, with its source and retrieval
date, in the same commit that adds it. A claim without a row is a defect.
