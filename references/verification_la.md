## Gap list

1. Whether Louisiana's disciplinary process for bar complaints has any published time limit on
   filing a complaint. Searched the LADB's "How to File a Complaint" page
   (<https://www.ladb.org/Complaint/HowTo.aspx>) directly; no deadline language was found. No
   other LADB or Louisiana Supreme Court rule on this point was located. Left out of the pack.

2. Whether Louisiana's attorney discipline process includes a restitution mechanism that can
   order a lawyer to return money as part of discipline. Searched the same LADB complaint
   page; the described discipline outcomes are dismissal, probation, admonition, formal
   charges, transfer to disability inactive status, a stay, or diversion, none of which are
   described as restitution. No separate LADB rule describing a restitution order was found.
   The pack states plainly that discipline is not confirmed to produce a refund, rather than
   asserting Louisiana has no such mechanism at all.

3. The specific filing deadline for a Client Assistance Fund claim. The fund's public page
   (<https://www.lsba.org/Public/ClientAssistance.aspx>) states "There are deadlines for
   starting this process" but does not say what they are, and its linked Application for
   Relief PDF does not state a deadline either. No separate LSBA rule governing the fund's
   procedures was located. Left out of the pack as an unspecified figure rather than guessed.

4. Whether the LSBA Client Assistance Fund pays awards in full or at a reduced percentage when
   fund resources are limited relative to claims. Neither the fund's public page nor its
   Application for Relief PDF addresses this. Left out of the pack.

5. Whether Louisiana's Rules of Professional Conduct (or any other primary Louisiana source)
   contain a Preamble or Scope section addressing whether a rule violation creates a private
   cause of action. The LADB-published booklet (154,881 characters of extracted text) was
   searched for "cause of action," "Preamble," and "Scope," and none of these produced a
   relevant section; the booklet appears to contain only the numbered rules, not preamble or
   scope material. No alternate primary source (a Louisiana Supreme Court case, the full LSBA
   Articles of Incorporation beyond Article XVI, or a Louisiana ethics opinion) was located
   and fetched within the scope of this pack's research. This is the pack's most significant
   gap and is stated as such in section 8.

6. Whether an attorney may fully represent a party inside a Small Claims Division proceeding,
   beyond the confirmed fact that a court may adopt a local rule limiting an attorney's role
   there under R.S. 13:5208. The Baton Rouge City Court's official small claims guide
   (brla.gov, dated 2-24-25) references restrictions on attorney participation that apply
   specifically within the small claims division, as distinct from the regular civil docket,
   but does not spell out exactly what those restrictions are in that court. No statewide
   default rule was found beyond the fact that local courts may set their own limit. The pack
   tells the reader to confirm the specific local rule rather than assuming full
   representation.

7. Whether New Orleans First and Second City Courts currently carry small claims
   jurisdictional limits different from the statewide $5,000 default. A 2020 legislative bill
   digest describes limits of $25,000 and $50,000, but that bill never passed the Senate and
   did not become law, and the current codified text of R.S. 13:5202, fetched directly for this
   pack, has no New Orleans-specific carve-out at all. Separately, R.S. 13:2151 and R.S.
   13:2151.4, the general (not small claims) civil jurisdiction statutes for these two courts,
   were fetched live and currently both read $50,000, under a 2026 amendment, not $25,000 and
   $50,000. No source found supports $25,000 or $50,000 as a small claims figure for either
   court. This is recorded in the Flagged table above and stated as unconfirmed in the pack
   rather than presented as settled.

8. The current small claims division filing fee. No statewide fee schedule was located; the
   pack tells the reader to confirm the fee with the specific city court rather than stating a
   figure.

9. Whether the fraud exception in R.S. 9:5605(E) lifts only the three-year outer peremptive
   cap or the one-year period as well. The statute text refers to "the peremptive period
   provided in Subsection A" without separately naming the one-year and three-year pieces. The
   opinion in *Lomont v. Myer-Bennett*, 2014-C-2483 (La. 6/30/15), was retrieved this time
   through a Justia case-law mirror and confirms that fraud removes all peremptive periods
   under R.S. 9:5605 and that the claim then falls under ordinary one-year prescription under
   Civil Code Article 3492, which, unlike peremption, can be interrupted or suspended. Because
   the source is a third-party mirror rather than lasc.org or an official reporter, this stays
   recorded as flagged rather than stated as settled law in the pack. Section 7 now says the
   statute text alone does not resolve which period the exception reaches, rather than
   asserting only the three-year period is excepted.

10. Whether Louisiana law imposes a statewide requirement that a lawyer notify a client of a
    right to fee arbitration before suing the client for fees. Searched the LSBA fee dispute
    program page, its Q&A page, and its Program Rules PDF; none states such a requirement.
    Section 3 states this as an unconfirmed absence rather than asserting no such rule exists
    anywhere in Louisiana law.
