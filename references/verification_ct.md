## Gap list

1. Looked for a Client Security Fund Committee regulation or published schedule stating a
   per-claim or aggregate dollar cap. The Committee's own Regulations page
   (jud.ct.gov/CSF/regs.htm) states payment amounts are set in the Committee's "sole and
   absolute discretion" based on claims approved, claims pending, and the fund's available
   balance, with no numeric maximum anywhere in the Regulations text. The FAQ page
   (jud.ct.gov/CSF/FAQ.htm) was also checked and states no dollar figure. Search stopped
   after reading both official pages in full; no cap is stated in the pack, and the pack
   presents this as a genuine absence rather than an unconfirmed number.
2. Looked for a filing deadline or limitations period specific to requesting CBA fee
   arbitration itself, as opposed to the general contract limitation period. The current
   CBA Lawyer-Client Fee Dispute Resolution Program Rules of Procedure PDF (Board of
   Governors, approved 05/22/23) was read in full; no filing deadline provision was found
   anywhere in it. Search stopped after reading the complete rules document and the CBA's
   public program page. The pack states this as unconfirmed rather than asserting a
   deadline.
3. Looked for whether Connecticut small claims filing fees or procedures vary by judicial
   district beyond the statewide dollar limits in General Statutes Sec. 51-15 and Practice
   Book chapter 24. Both sources set statewide figures with no district-level variation
   named in the text itself. No district-by-district comparison was attempted. The pack
   states this was not confirmed rather than asserting statewide uniformity beyond what the
   statute and Practice Book actually say.
4. Looked for whether a corporate party is required to appear through counsel in Connecticut
   small claims specifically. Practice Book chapter 24 and General Statutes chapter 870 were
   both read in full; neither states a counsel requirement for any party type. This silence
   is treated as inconclusive rather than as confirmation that no such requirement exists
   anywhere in Connecticut practice, and the pack says so.
5. Did not independently fetch or read the full text of the underlying court opinions cited
   in Connecticut's own statutory annotations: 76 CA 599 (executed versus executory oral
   contracts), 263 C. 588 (continuous representation doctrine), and 89 CA 690 (malpractice
   claim not ripe until final judgment in the underlying matter). All three are cited only
   through the state's own official annotated General Statutes (cga.ct.gov), which this kit
   treats as a primary source for the existence and general holding of each case, matching
   the treatment of Acharya v. Carroll and Hennekens v. Hoerl in verification_wi.md. The
   opinions themselves were not confirmed via CourtListener or another case database; its
   opinion-text endpoint was not reachable during this session's research (metadata search

   may hold a client's file as a retaining lien against unpaid fees, the same question

   comment was found attached to Connecticut's Rule 1.16 text in the Practice Book as
   fetched. This is omitted from the pack rather than asserted either way.
7. Looked for whether Connecticut's fee arbitration or grievance filing deadlines have been
   amended more recently than the CBA Rules of Procedure's Board of Governors approval date
   of May 22, 2023, or the Practice Book's stated Rule 1.5 amendment of October 1, 2013. The

   for either provision cited in this pack. No separate amendment-history search beyond the
