## Gap list

1. Colorado small claims filing fee: no statute or fee schedule giving the current dollar amount
   was located. Part 4 of C.R.S. Article 6 (sections 13-6-401 through 13-6-415) does not itself
   state a filing fee figure. The search did not extend to the separate court fee schedule
   statutes (C.R.S. 13-32-101 et seq.) or the state judicial branch's own published fee schedule.
   The pack tells a reader to confirm the fee with the county court clerk instead of stating a
   number.
2. County-level variation in the small claims dollar limit was not found and is not believed to
   exist; the limit in C.R.S. 13-6-403 is set at the state level with no county-by-county
   provision seen in the statute text reviewed. This is stated in the pack as a confirmed
   absence within the material reviewed, not an exhaustive survey of every county's local rules.
3. A statute of repose specific to legal malpractice was searched for and not found. The
   4-year figure commonly attributed to C.R.S. 13-80-102.5 was traced to that statute's actual
   text and found to apply only to health-care institutions and licensed health-care
   professionals as the statute itself defines them, not to attorneys. No other Colorado repose
   provision for legal malpractice was located in the CRS Title 13 PDF reviewed for this pack.
   This claim was cut entirely rather than shipped on the strength of the source that
   originated it.
4. Whether a Colorado legal malpractice claim is available in contract, in tort, or at the
   plaintiff's election, and which limitation period (2-year tort or 3-year contract) applies to
   a given set of facts, was not resolved. The pack states both periods and the discovery rule
   and leaves the theory choice to a reader's own malpractice counsel, consistent with this kit
   not covering malpractice strategy.
5. Whether a rules violation is admissible as standard-of-care evidence in an actual Colorado
   malpractice case beyond the CBA's own annotation (see the flagged table) was not researched
   further; the underlying case, Moye White LLP v. Beren, was not independently read.
6. The Colorado small claims filing fee statute series (C.R.S. Title 13, Article 32) and any
   service-of-process fee under C.R.S. 13-32-104(1)(i), referenced only in passing in the OARC
   materials reviewed for an unrelated point, was not separately fetched or confirmed.
7. Whether Colorado recognizes fee forfeiture as a distinct remedy for a lawyer's serious breach
   of duty, separate from fee arbitration, discipline, or the Client Protection Fund, was not
   researched.
8. Local or county bar association fee dispute programs separate from the statewide CBA/Denver
   Bar LFAC were not surveyed. Only the LFAC's own materials and OARC's public guidance were
   reviewed.
9. The secondary statutes cross-referenced inside Colo. RPC 1.5's contingent fee form language,
   C.R.S. 6-1-737 (consumer pricing disclosure) and C.R.S. 8-43-403 (workers' compensation
   contingent fee cap), were not independently researched or confirmed, and no claim about them
   appears in the pack.
10. Whether Colorado's LexisNexis-hosted "hottopics" statute portal, the link the Colorado
    General Assembly's own website designates as its free public statute access point, is
    reachable without a subscription was tested directly and found not to be: it returns a
    client-side cookie/JavaScript verification shell that redirects to an authenticated
    advance.lexis.com sign-in page. The Colorado General Assembly's own directly downloadable
    Title 13 PDF, cited throughout the confirmed table above, was used instead and required no
    login.
11. Total dollars paid out historically by the Attorneys' Fund for Client Protection, a figure
    mentioned in passing in OARC's FAQ ("as of December 31, 2012, the fund has paid out $5.1
    million"), is stale by more than a decade and was not restated in the pack as a current
    figure.
12. Two URLs previously cited in this file, <https://cobar.org/For-Members/Committees/Legal-Fee-Arbitration-Committee>
    and <https://www.coloradolegalregulation.com/public/feedisputes/>, were independently
    re-fetched on 2026-08-20 and found to return only navigation chrome, not the substantive
    text the rows attributed to them (confirmed on the raw bytes, not just stripped text; also
    confirmed against Wayback Machine snapshots of both URLs from 2021 and December 2025, which
    were equally empty of the claim text). Both are DotNetNuke-rendered pages whose real content
    loads through a client-side module the crawler never executes. The six LFAC rows were
    re-sourced to the CBA's own Colorado Lawyer magazine article at
    <https://cl.cobar.org/features/legal-fee-dispute-arbitration-through-the-cba/> (dated April
    2022, authored by the LFAC's own former co-chair), and the OARC-tagline and complaint-filing
    rows were re-sourced to the OARC Filing-a-Complaint page at
    <https://www.coloradolegalregulation.com/complaints/file_complaintagainstatty/>. Neither of
    the two broken URLs is cited anywhere in this file or in state_co.md any longer.
13. CourtListener's search API, <https://www.courtlistener.com/api/rest/v4/search/?q=Moye+White+Beren&type=o>,
    was fetched on 2026-08-20 to check whether Moye White LLP v. Beren, 2013 COA 89, is a real,
    correctly cited case. The API returned a result confirming a Colorado Court of Appeals
    opinion matching that case name and year. The opinion's own text was not reachable: the
    text endpoint returns 401 and the HTML case page returns 202 with an empty body on this
    machine. So the citation itself is confirmed to exist and to be correctly identified; the
    holding described in the CBA's annotation remains unconfirmed against the opinion's actual
    text and stays in the flagged table for that reason.

## How to extend this file

Add a row to the confirmed table for any new claim, with its source URL and retrieval date,
before adding the claim to state_co.md. If the best available source is not primary or
official, add the claim to the flagged table with a reason instead. If no adequate source can
be found, add the item to the gap list and leave it out of the pack.
