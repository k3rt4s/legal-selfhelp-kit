# Verification: Alaska (state_ak.md)

Every claim in state_ak.md traced to its source below. Retrieved 2026-08-20 unless noted.
All Alaska Rules of Professional Conduct and Alaska Bar Rules citations were fetched
directly from the Alaska Court System's own PDF rule books at courts.alaska.gov/rules,
using a Chrome user agent and a Google/courts.alaska.gov referer, then text-extracted with
pypdf and the specific rule heading and operative text located before being treated as
confirmed. AS 09.10 and AS 22.15 statute text was fetched from akleg.gov's own AJAX statute
endpoint (statutes.asp with type=fetch&secStart=... and media=print, sent with an
X-Requested-With header), which returns the Legislature's own current codified text rather
than the JavaScript-gated search page front end; the returned body was grepped for each
section number and read in full.

This file was independently re-verified on 2026-08-20 by re-fetching every PDF and statute
section named below from scratch, rather than trusting the drafter's own account of what it
found. One claim moved from Flagged to Confirmed (the Sheldon-Lee case, now read from the
Alaska Court System's own opinion document); one claim moved from the gap list to Confirmed
(the Client Protection Fund's three-year filing deadline, found inside Rule 45(f)(4)); and
the attorney's-lien gap entry was sharpened with named case law the drafter had not searched
for, without asserting a holding this session did not read in full.

## Gap list

1. Attorney's retaining lien for unpaid fees. Rule 1.16(d) says a lawyer may retain a
   client's papers "to the extent permitted by other law," and Rule 1.8(i)(1) allows a
   lien "authorized by law," but this kit did not locate a specific Alaska statute
   establishing a common-law attorney's retaining lien, or its scope. A search of Alaska
   Statutes Title 34 (Property) around the mechanics'/wage-lien statutes (AS 34.35.430 and
   nearby sections) turned up wage and materialman's liens, not an attorney's lien. This
   session went one step further than the drafter and ran a CourtListener case search for
   `"attorney's lien" court:alaska`, which returned several Alaska Supreme Court cases that
   use that term, including Sheehan v. Estate of Gamberg, 677 P.2d 254 (Alaska 1984), and
   Law Offices of Vincent Vitale, P.C. v. Tabbytite, 942 P.2d 1141 (Alaska 1997). This
   confirms the search terms exist in Alaska case law; it does not confirm what the doctrine
   actually holds, because this session did not read those opinions in full to state a
   holding, and a search hit is not a source. A future session should read Sheehan and
   Vitale directly before stating anything about the scope of an Alaska attorney's lien.
   Until then, section 2's answer is limited to what the rule text itself says, and a reader
   should ask their own counsel or the Bar directly rather than assume either answer.
2. Fee arbitration petition filing fee. Bar Rules 36 and 40 describe the petition and
   review process but do not mention a filing fee for the petition itself. This kit does
   not assert a fee amount, including "no fee," beyond what was read; a reader should
   confirm directly with the Alaska Bar Association's Fee Dispute Resolution Program.
3. Informal, non-disciplinary consumer-assistance track. Some other states (for example,
   Utah) run a distinct pre-grievance consumer assistance program for informal help before
   a formal grievance. This kit did not find an equivalent named program on the Alaska Bar
   Association's website pages reviewed (alaskabar.org's "Complaints Against Attorneys"
   and related pages returned mostly navigation menu content when fetched and stripped of
   HTML, with the substantive body text not captured); the informal/formal distinction
   described in section 4 is the private-admonition-versus-public-hearing choice inside
   the formal grievance process itself, not a separate pre-complaint program.
4. Whether AS 09.10.053 (three-year contract period) or the Sheldon-Lee decision's
   reasoning is the better authority to cite for a written fee agreement dispute that is
   purely contractual and does not sound in malpractice at all. Section 7 states the
   general contract period from the statute directly, which should cover a pure fee
   dispute; it does not attempt to extend the malpractice-specific continuous
   representation rule to a non-malpractice contract claim, since that was not confirmed.
5. This kit's small claims Rule 15(b) confirms a lawyer may appear, but does not address
   whether Alaska small claims practice discourages or otherwise limits attorney
   involvement in ways not stated in the rule text itself (for example, informal court
   culture or judge practice). Only the rule as written is stated in section 6.
