## Gap list

   lawyer as a contract claim, reaching the six-year period under ORS 12.080, instead
   of a tort claim under the two-year period of ORS 12.110. Checked the text of both
   statutes directly and the official annotations to ORS 12.110, ano012.html. Neither
   states a general election rule for legal malpractice claims. This is omitted from
   the pack; section 7 states only that this was not confirmed rather than asserting an
   election right.

   distinct from the medical/surgical/dental-specific five-year repose in ORS

   specific repose provision was found. This is omitted from the pack; section 7 states
   only the two-year discovery-rule period, with no confirmed repose limit.

8. Looked for a Preamble or Scope section in the Oregon Rules of Professional Conduct
   addressing whether a rule violation creates a private cause of action, the kind of
   statement several other states' rules carry. A full-text search of the compiled ORPC
   PDF found no Preamble, no general Scope section, and the single "cause of action"
   hit in the document is the unrelated malpractice-arbitration-consent provision at
   ORPC 1.8(h)(3). The table of contents begins directly at Rule 1.0. This absence is
   recorded in section 8 of the pack directly rather than treated as a silent gap,
   because the search itself, not just its failure, is the finding: the OSB-published
