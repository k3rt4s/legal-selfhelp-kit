## Gap list

This is the most trustworthy part of this file: it says exactly where the pack stops knowing
things, rather than guessing or padding past a dead end.

1. Whether North Dakota courts have applied a judicial discovery rule to legal malpractice
   claims against attorneys, separate from the statutory discovery rule that N.D.C.C. section
   28-01-18(3) limits by its own text to claims against a physician or licensed hospital. No
   case law search was performed. The pack states the flat two-year statutory period for
   malpractice and does not assert any discovery rule or repose period for it, because none was
   found in the statute itself and none was independently confirmed in case law.

2. Whether North Dakota separates an informal or consumer-assistance complaint track from the
   formal Disciplinary Board process, the way some states do. The ndcourts.gov Disciplinary
   Board page and SBAND's "For Public" and "File a Complaint" pages were both read, and both
   describe a single pathway through the Office of Disciplinary Counsel with no separate
   informal track named. This is stated in the pack as what was found, not as a certainty that
   no informal option exists anywhere in SBAND's practice; ask the Office of Disciplinary
   Counsel directly if this matters to you.

3. Whether the SBAND fee arbitration program charges any filing fee. None is stated in the
   Rules on Arbitration of Fee Disputes document itself, and no separate fee schedule for the
   program was found. This is stated as an absence in what was searched, not a guarantee that
   no fee will be charged.

4. This item previously flagged an unreached live source for the SBAND Client Protection Fund
   rules. On a second attempt, following the live sband.org site's own navigation reached a
   current PDF (<https://www.sband.org/resource/resmgr/docs/for_public/CPFRules.pdf>) that is
   substantively identical to the 2008/2014 document used throughout this pack, including the
   same two-year/five-year deadline conflict. A reader relying on any client-protection-fund
   figure in this pack should still confirm it directly with SBAND, P.O. Box 2136, Bismarck, ND
   58502-2136, since a bar-association rule can be amended at any time.

5. Whether North Dakota's Rules for Lawyer Discipline contain a time limit on filing a
   complaint anywhere outside the five specific rules reviewed (1.3, 3.5, 4.3, 6.3, 6.4). Those
   five were read directly and none contains a deadline or a laches provision; the full rule set
   was not read end to end, and no North Dakota case addressing timeliness of a disciplinary
   complaint (comparable to Tennessee's *Long v. Board of Professional Responsibility*) was
   found or cited.

6. N.D.C.C. sections 28-01-15 and 41-02-104, cross-referenced inside the six-year contract
   limitation at section 28-01-16(1), were not independently read. If your contract claim
   involves a sale of goods or a specialized contract type, one of these cross-referenced
   sections could set a different period; this pack states the general six-year rule only.

7. Whether a fee arbitration award confirmed as a judgment under N.D.C.C. ch. 32-29.3 can later
   be challenged on grounds such as fraud or arbitrator misconduct. Chapter 32-29.3 itself was
   not read for this pack; only the fee arbitration rules' own reference to it was used.

8. The SBAND fee arbitration rules document names two regional panels covering only seven of
   North Dakota's eight current judicial districts (Southeast, East Central, Northeast Central,
   Northeast, South Central, Southwest, Northwest); the North Central Judicial District, created
   by N.D. Admin. Rule 6 effective 8/11/2021, is not named in either panel's district list. No
   newer or corrected version of the arbitration rules document could be found: a Wayback CDX
   search returned only one prior snapshot from 2022, itself blocked by a Cloudflare challenge,
   and the live PDF was byte-for-byte identical to a cdn.ymaws.com mirror copy. This pack states
   the panel structure as written in the source document and does not guess which panel would
   actually handle a North Central District dispute; a reader in that district should ask SBAND
   directly which panel applies.
