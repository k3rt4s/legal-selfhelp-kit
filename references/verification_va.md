## Gap list

1. Whether a proven Rules of Professional Conduct violation is admissible as evidence of the
   standard of care in a Virginia legal malpractice case was not researched. The kit does not
   cover malpractice strategy, so this was not pursued past the limitation-period deadlines
   needed to tell a reader a clock exists. The Bar's own Scope section, unlike the ABA model
   rule's Scope section, was not found to include the model's express qualifier that a violation
   "may be used as evidence" of a standard of care; its absence from the text extracted for this
   pack was noted but not treated as a confirmed finding that Virginia rejects that use, only as
   a gap.
2. General District Court civil filing fee, and Small Claims Division filing fee specifically, as
   single statewide dollar figures. See the flagged table above; search stopped at the Court
   System's own fee calculator rather than working through the underlying fee statutes
   (Va. Code § 17.1-272 and related sections) claim type by claim type.
3. Whether a violation of the Rules of Professional Conduct creates a private cause of action in
   Virginia beyond the Scope section's own general statement that it does not. No separate
   Virginia case or statute confirming or narrowing that Scope-section language was sought; the
   Scope section's own text was treated as sufficient to answer the template's question, the same
   approach the Tennessee pack in this kit took.
4. Whether Virginia's disciplinary rule (Part Six, Section IV, Paragraph 13) affirmatively states
   there is no time limit for filing a complaint, as opposed to simply not stating one, was not
   resolved. The 72-page rule text was searched for "time limit," "statute of limitations," and
   related terms with no matches; this pack reports that absence rather than asserting a
   holding, case, or rule provision saying explicitly that no deadline exists. A secondary source
   found during research claimed the Supreme Court of Virginia settled this in 2010, but no
   primary source (opinion or rule amendment) for that claim was located, so it was omitted from
   the pack entirely.
5. County-level or circuit-level variation in the General District Court's $4,500 and $50,000
   jurisdictional figures, or in the Small Claims Division's $5,000 figure, was checked against
   the statute text directly and none was found; the statutes set these figures uniformly
   statewide with no county carve-out provision. This is stated as a confirmed finding in the
   pack, not a gap, but is noted here because a Tennessee-style "no county exception, confirmed
   by corroborating county sources" cross-check against individual circuit court pages was not
   performed the way it was for Tennessee's General Sessions figure.
6. Any Virginia-specific rule, beyond the ordinary jurisdictional statutes cited in Section 6,
   governing a suit specifically against a lawyer in General District Court was searched for and
   not found. No further search was done beyond the statutes already cited.
7. No dollar figure for what any particular remedy typically recovers, in any forum in this pack.
   There is no verified dataset behind such a number, and none was sought.
8. Whether Rule 1.5(e)'s fee-division provision requires proportional division or joint
   responsibility for the matter, the way the ABA model rule does, was read in the compiled rules
   text but is not included in the pack because the national baseline this pack sits on top of
   does not itself cover Model Rule 1.5(e), so no comparison point exists in this kit to describe
   a deviation against. Noted here rather than included in the pack as an unanchored claim.
