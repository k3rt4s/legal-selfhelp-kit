## Gap list

1. Whether Maryland used a different rule-numbering scheme (for example, a "Rule 16-812" style
   number) before the July 1, 2016 adoption of the current MARPC. A CourtListener search turned
   up only case search metadata (case names and dates) from a 2009 and a 2018 Court of Appeals
   opinion, with no readable snippet actually containing any pre-2016 rule number. That is too
   weak to state as fact, so it was cut from the pack entirely rather than flagged.
2. The Preamble and Scope section of the MARPC, Rule 19-300.1, where most states place the
   "rules are not a basis for civil liability" language. Three separate fetch attempts to
   <https://govt.westlaw.com/mdc/Document/N35AB9FE03C0211E6ACAF9E5216076AB4> each returned "Content
   not available - This content isn't in this site's subscription plan," despite the same host
   successfully serving Rules 19-301.4, 19-301.5, and 19-301.16 in the same session. No other
   primary Maryland source for this section's text was found in the time available.
3. Whether Maryland's Attorney Grievance Commission runs a separate informal or
   consumer-assistance intake track apart from the formal complaint process, whether there is any
   time limit on filing a complaint, and whether Maryland has a disciplinary restitution
   mechanism. The AGC's complaint and FAQ pages were fetched and read but did not address these
   points directly.
4. Any filing deadline or filing fee for MSBA Fee Dispute Committee arbitration, and whether the
   lawyer must notify the client of a right to arbitrate before suing for fees. The Committee's
   regulations, fetched and read in full, did not state either point.
5. What the four carved-out local fee dispute programs, Baltimore City, Baltimore County,
   Montgomery County, and Prince George's County, actually do. Attempts to reach the Baltimore
   County Bar Association and the Prince George's County Bar Association sites failed with DNS
   errors, and the Montgomery County Bar Association site did not yield usable fee-dispute
   procedure content in the time available.
6. Whether a loss caused by a lawyer's negligence, short of malpractice, is excluded from the
   Client Protection Fund. The fund's own FAQ page states plainly that it does not handle
   malpractice claims and draws a line between fee disputes and theft, but neither the FAQ page
   nor the fund's regulations PDF, both fetched and read in full, uses the word "negligence"
   anywhere. Also unresolved: whether an Attorney Grievance Commission complaint or finding is a
   strict filing prerequisite for a fund claim; the fund's regulations and FAQ page were read but
   did not state this plainly enough to confirm.
7. A Maryland limitation period specific to legal malpractice that differs from the general
   three-year period in CJP section 5-101, and whether Maryland treats a malpractice claim as
   sounding in tort, contract, or either at the client's election. No dedicated source for this
   distinction was reached in the time available.
8. The exact District Court small claims filing fee dollar amount. The court's self-help page
   points to its own Civil Cost Schedule, form DCA-109, rather than stating a figure, and that
   schedule itself was not fetched.
