## Gap list

1. No dollar figure for a magisterial district court or Philadelphia Municipal Court filing
   fee. Fee schedules vary by claim amount and by county, and no official court fee schedule
   was reached during this research; the pack points the reader to the court directly instead
   of stating a number. Search stopped at non-official summary sites after an official schedule
   could not be located.
2. No filing deadline for a Pennsylvania Disciplinary Board complaint was found on the Board's
   own complaint-filing page. The pack does not state a deadline either way. Search stopped at
   padisciplinaryboard.org's file-complaint page, which does not address a time limit.
3. No filing deadline for a Pennsylvania Lawyers Fund for Client Security claim, whether tied
   to the date of loss or its discovery, was found on the Fund's own FAQ, "what type of claim,"
   or "how do I file" pages. Search stopped there; a phone call to the Fund would be the next
   step for a reader who needs this answered before filing.
4. Whether a proven Rule of Professional Conduct violation is admissible as evidence of the
   standard of care in a Pennsylvania legal malpractice case was not researched past locating
   the Scope section's bar on a private cause of action. The kit does not cover malpractice
   strategy, so this was not pursued further.
5. Filing deadlines, dollar ceilings or floors, and filing fees for the Chester County,
   Montgomery County, and Dauphin County fee dispute programs beyond what is stated in the pack
   were not located on those programs' own public pages. Each program's page describes the
   voluntary, non-appealable nature of the process but not a numeric deadline or fee schedule.
   Ask each program directly.
6. A statute of repose specific to legal malpractice, separate from the discovery rule, was not
   located for Pennsylvania. Search stopped after Bailey v. Tucker and Fine v. Checcio, neither
   of which describes a fixed outer limit independent of discovery, unlike Tennessee's five-year
   repose period. This pack does not claim Pennsylvania has no repose period; it states only
   that none was found.
7. Whether other Pennsylvania counties beyond the seven listed in Section 3 run their own fee
   dispute programs was not fully determined. The seven listed are the ones whose own program
   pages could be reached and read. Pennsylvania has 67 counties; the absence of a listed
   program for any other county is not a finding that no program exists there, only that this
   research did not find and read one. Ask the local county bar association directly.
8. padisciplinaryboard.org's own consolidated PDF of the current Rules of Professional Conduct
   (dated November 14, 2024 in its file name as of this pack's retrieval) could not be read as
   text by this pack's tooling; pacodeandbulletin.gov was used instead for every rule quoted.
   Whether the two publications differ in any way was not checked.
