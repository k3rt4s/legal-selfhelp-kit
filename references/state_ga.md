# Georgia

This pack is for a person in Georgia who has a fee, billing, communication, or file-return
dispute with their own lawyer. It does not cover legal malpractice as a claim to run yourself.
Retrieved 2026-08-20.

What a Georgia reader most needs to know, that a reader in another state does not:

- Georgia runs a single, statewide fee arbitration program through the State Bar itself, but
  it is voluntary on both sides. Filing a petition binds you to the result; it does not bind
  the lawyer unless the lawyer separately agrees to be bound too.
- Georgia's Rule 1.5 attaches its own maximum penalty, a public reprimand, directly to the
  rule. Several individual Georgia rules work this way, unlike the ABA model, which leaves
  penalties to a separate disciplinary process.
- Georgia's Rule 1.5(b) has a real, specific requirement the model rule does not: an
  arbitration clause inside a fee agreement must sit in its own paragraph, in a font as large
  as the rest of the contract, and be separately initialed by both the client and the lawyer.
- The informal complaint intake point is called the Client Assistance Program, not a "consumer"
  assistance program. A formal grievance is a separate, signed document filed with the Office
  of the General Counsel.
- Georgia's Clients' Security Fund pays only for a lawyer's dishonest conduct, not incompetence
  or a bad outcome, and only after the lawyer has been disbarred, suspended indefinitely, has
  surrendered their license, or cannot be found.

## 1. Rules of professional conduct, numbering and adoption

Georgia adopts the Georgia Rules of Professional Conduct as Part IV of the Rules and
Regulations of the State Bar of Georgia. Cite them as "Rule" followed by the number, for
example "Rule 1.5," the same numbering a reader would expect from the ABA model rules. The
rules a client is most likely to need sit at the numbers you would expect: Rule 1.4
(communication), Rule 1.5 (fees), Rule 1.15 (safekeeping property), and Rule 1.16 (declining or
terminating representation).

The official publication point is the State Bar of Georgia's own online rules handbook, at
<https://www.gabar.org/general-counsel/georgia-rules-of-professional-conduct>. The current
Rules of Professional Conduct were adopted by the Supreme Court of Georgia; the exact date of
adoption or the text of the adopting order was not located in this pack's research, so no
effective date is stated here. See [verification_ga.md](verification_ga.md) for what was
searched.

A structural feature worth knowing: Georgia writes a maximum penalty directly into many
individual rules, rather than leaving penalty entirely to a separate disciplinary process. Rule
1.5 and Rule 1.16 each carry a stated maximum penalty of a public reprimand. That is the
ceiling for a Rule 1.5 or Rule 1.16 violation alone; other rules carry harsher stated maximums,
including disbarment for some.

## 2. Deviations from the model reasonableness factors

Georgia's Rule 1.5(a) lists the same eight reasonableness factors as the ABA model rule, in the
same order, with no factor added or removed. If you are comparing a bill against these factors,
the national baseline's list applies directly; nothing in Georgia's count is different.

The real deviations sit elsewhere in Rule 1.5:

- Rule 1.5(b) requires the scope of representation and the basis or rate of the fee to be
  communicated to the client, preferably in writing, before or within a reasonable time after
  the representation begins, matching the model rule. Georgia adds a specific requirement for
  arbitration clauses: to the extent an agreement to arbitrate fee disputes is enforceable, it
  requires the client's informed consent in writing, and the arbitration clause itself must be
  set out in its own separate paragraph, in a font size at least as large as the rest of the
  contract, and separately initialed by both the client and the lawyer. A clause buried in fine
  print inside the main body of the agreement, without a separate initial, does not meet this
  requirement.
- Rule 1.5's maximum penalty is written into the rule itself: a public reprimand. This is a
  genuine structural difference from the ABA model, which does not attach penalties to
  individual rules at all.
- Rule 1.16(d) lists the same four steps as the model rule on discharge or withdrawal: giving
  reasonable notice, allowing time to hire other counsel, surrendering papers and property the
  client is entitled to, and refunding any advance fee payment that was not earned. Georgia
  does not expand this into a longer list the way some states do. Rule 1.16(d)'s maximum
  penalty is also a public reprimand.
- Contingent fees are prohibited in the same two situations as the model rule: a domestic
  relations matter where the fee or its amount depends on securing a divorce or on the amount
  of alimony or support, and representing a defendant in a criminal case. No Georgia-specific
  widening, narrowing, or dollar cap on contingent fees generally was found; see the gap list in
  [verification_ga.md](verification_ga.md).

## 3. Attorney fee arbitration

Georgia runs one statewide program: the State Bar of Georgia's Committee on the Arbitration of
Attorney Fee Disputes, under Part VI of the Bar's own rules (Rules 6-101 through 6-423), reached
through the Bar's Fee Arbitration program page at
<https://www.gabar.org/programs/fee-arbitration>. This is
a State Bar program, not a court, and Georgia's bar is unified and mandatory, so every
practicing lawyer is a member subject to it.

Participation is voluntary on both sides, not mandatory on the lawyer. Filing a petition binds
the person who filed it to the arbitration's result. If a client files and the lawyer refuses
to be bound, the matter is not accepted for arbitration at all. The award, once both sides are
bound, is final and binding. This means the program only works when both people agree to use
it; a lawyer who wants to keep the option of suing in court, or defending a suit there, can
decline.

To be accepted, the dispute must meet several conditions: the fee was for legal services by a
lawyer who is or was a State Bar of Georgia member (or otherwise authorized to practice in
Georgia), the services were performed in Georgia or from a Georgia office, and the disputed fee
exceeds $1,000. The petition must be filed no more than 2 years after the date the dispute
arose. A respondent has 20 calendar days after being served with the petition to file
an answer. Arbitrators serve without pay unless every party to the dispute is a lawyer, in which
case the Committee may direct that they be compensated by the lawyer parties. The panel aims to
issue an award within 30 days of the hearing's close. If both sides are bound, an award under
the Georgia Arbitration Code (OCGA § 9-9-1 et seq.) is enforceable in court like any other
arbitration award.

If you also sue the lawyer in court over the same facts, including a malpractice claim, the
Committee can suspend or terminate the arbitration rather than run both processes at once.

No rule was found requiring a Georgia lawyer to notify a client of the right to arbitrate
before suing the client for fees. This kit does not assert such a requirement exists; see the
gap list in [verification_ga.md](verification_ga.md).

If the fee arbitration program will take your case, it is usually cheaper and faster than
court, and it does not require a lawyer to represent you. If the lawyer will not agree to be
bound, or the dispute is under $1,000, the realistic path is a written demand followed by
magistrate court (Section 6).

## 4. Bar complaint process

The Office of the General Counsel (OGC) of the State Bar of Georgia is the disciplinary
authority; see <https://www.gabar.org/general-counsel/ethics---discipline>. It works alongside
the Client Assistance Program (CAP), which is the public's informal intake point, distinct from
a formal grievance. A formal grievance is filed through the Bar's own File a Grievance page at
<https://www.gabar.org/general-counsel/file-a-grievance>.

A formal grievance is a written, signed Memorandum of Grievance filed with OGC. An informal
complaint goes through CAP's own Intake Form; CAP can direct a complaining party to appropriate
resources, and OGC may refer a matter to CAP instead of forwarding it toward formal discipline
when it does not present enough to proceed as a rule violation. OGC can also open an
investigation on credible information from any source other than a grievance, but must first
notify the lawyer and describe the basis for the investigation. A billing disagreement, without
more, is more likely to be handled through CAP than through a formal grievance that goes
nowhere.

Discipline under Rule 4-102(b) has six levels, from most to least severe: disbarment,
suspension, public reprimand, State Disciplinary Review Board reprimand, State Disciplinary
Board reprimand, and formal admonition. The Supreme Court of Georgia imposes the most serious
public discipline.

No deadline for filing a grievance or complaint was located in the rules read for this pack,
and no Georgia case confirming or denying that laches applies to attorney discipline was found.
Do not assume there is or is not a deadline; see the gap list in
[verification_ga.md](verification_ga.md).

Discipline is not a refund. It puts the lawyer's conduct on record, but getting money back runs
through fee arbitration (Section 3), a contract claim (Section 6), or the Clients' Security Fund
where the conduct was dishonest (Section 5).

## 5. Client protection fund

Georgia's fund is the Clients' Security Fund of the State Bar of Georgia, established and
governed by Rules 10-101 through 10-112, Part X of the Bar's own rules; see
<https://www.gabar.org/general-counsel/clients-security-fund>. It is a public service
of the legal profession, funded by an annual assessment of $15 per dues-paying lawyer, not a
client remedy you pay into and not malpractice insurance.

What it covers: a loss caused by a lawyer's dishonest conduct, meaning theft, embezzlement, or
the wrongful taking or conversion of money, property, or other things of value, arising out of
a lawyer-client or fiduciary relationship. The fund does not reimburse losses from malpractice
or incompetence, only from dishonesty.

To qualify, there must be a final disposition against the lawyer: indefinite suspension,
disbarment, or voluntary surrender of license, or, if that cannot be pursued because the lawyer
is deceased, disabled, or cannot be located after a diligent search, the fund can still
consider the claim under a different deadline (below).

Excluded losses include: losses to the lawyer's spouse, children, parents, grandparents,
siblings, partners, associates, or employees; losses already covered by a bond, insurance, or
surety contract; losses to a financial institution recoverable under a banker's blanket bond;
losses to a business entity the lawyer controls; losses to a government entity; and losses to a
corporation or partnership. The Board has discretion to recognize an otherwise-excluded claim
in a case of extreme hardship, and discretion to deny a claim involving unjust enrichment or
the claimant's own contribution to the loss.

Two filing deadlines, and which applies depends on the case:

1. The ordinary rule: file within 2 years after the Supreme Court of Georgia's final
   disciplinary action against the lawyer.
2. Where discipline cannot be pursued because the lawyer is deceased, disabled, or cannot be
   located: file within 5 years after you discovered the dishonest conduct, but never later than
   7 years after the conduct itself occurred, whichever is earlier in practice.

You must generally exhaust your available civil remedies against the lawyer before the fund
will pay, unless the Board finds that pursuing them is not feasible or practical.

There is no published fixed maximum award per claim. Payment is a matter of the Board's
discretion, described in the rule itself as "a matter of grace and not of right," and the Board
weighs the fund's resources and the priority of competing claims. A denial or the amount of an
award can be appealed for reconsideration within 30 days of notice; after that, the Board's
decision is final.

## 6. Small claims or equivalent court

Georgia's small claims equivalent is magistrate court, governed by OCGA Title 15, Chapter 10.
There are no jury trials in magistrate court. The civil jurisdictional limit is $15,000.
Magistrate court cannot grant a prejudgment attachment at all, whatever the amount, so it is
not a forum for freezing a lawyer's assets before you have a judgment.

Effective July 1, 2023, Georgia replaced the old notice-of-appeal procedure from magistrate
court and other lower judicatories with a "petition for review," filed in the superior or state
court of the county, under OCGA §§ 5-3-1 through 5-3-21. The petition must be filed within 30
days after the lower court's final judgment is filed or, if notice of the judgment was not
provided to all parties, within 30 days after notice is provided (OCGA § 5-3-7(b)). The appeal
is a new hearing in the reviewing court, not a review limited to the magistrate's record.

No filing fee figure was located; magistrate court filing fees vary by county. Confirm the
current fee and any local rules with the magistrate court clerk in the county where you plan to
file.

## 7. Statutes of limitation

A fee agreement dispute, such as a lawyer's failure to refund an unearned fee, is a contract
claim. Georgia's statute of limitations is 6 years for a written contract (OCGA § 9-3-24) and
4 years for an oral contract or open account (OCGA § 9-3-25), each subject to a separate
carve-out for the sale of goods that does not usually apply to a legal fee dispute.

Legal malpractice, a claim that the lawyer's own negligence caused you harm, needs its own
counsel rather than self-help. It is not covered further in this kit beyond the deadline,
included so you know a clock exists and can get counsel in time. Georgia applies a 4-year
period under OCGA § 9-3-25 to legal malpractice claims. Georgia does not apply a discovery
rule: the clock starts running immediately at the wrongful act, not when you learned of the
injury or its extent. Georgia also does not apply a continuous representation rule that would
otherwise pause the clock while the same lawyer keeps representing you. A malpractice claim can
already be time-barred even if you only recently discovered the problem, so do not wait to
consult a malpractice lawyer if you suspect one.

## 8. Private right of action for a rules violation

Georgia's own rules say directly, in the comment to Rule 1.1, that the purpose of the Rules of
Professional Conduct is not to give rise to a cause of action or to create a presumption that a
legal duty was breached; they exist to guide lawyers and structure discipline, not to serve as
a basis for civil liability. A rules violation supports a grievance and can inform a fee
arbitration, but it is not by itself a claim for money damages.

Separately, the Georgia Supreme Court has held that a mandatory arbitration clause in a law
firm's engagement agreement is not automatically void as against public policy or unconscionable,
even where it would cover a future malpractice claim, and it declined to decide whether an
attorney's failure to explain the clause violated Rule 1.4(b), leaving that question to the
Bar's own disciplinary process rather than treating it as grounds to void the agreement.

Your money route runs through the other forums in this pack: fee arbitration (Section 3), a
contract claim in magistrate court or another court (Section 6), or the Clients' Security Fund
where the conduct was dishonest rather than merely a bad bill (Section 5). Getting the lawyer
disciplined (Section 4) is a separate goal from getting money back, and it runs through a
separate forum.
