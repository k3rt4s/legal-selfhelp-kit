it was retrieved, and how far it should be trusted. If a claim is not on this list, it is not
sourced, and it should not be in the pack.

directly; nothing reported by an earlier draft was taken on trust. Retrieval date for
everything below is 2026-08-20.

## Cut from the pack (could not be sourced to a primary or official publication)

1. The claim that the current Georgia Rules of Professional Conduct "took effect January 1,
   2001" was removed from Section 1. The only support found was a WebSearch synthesis of
   secondary sources, plus the indirect clue that the State Bar's own handbook labels a prior
   rule set "Part III (After January 1, 2001) - Reserved." Neither is the adopting order itself,
   and no adopting order, docket number, or dated official notice stating the effective date
   directly was located after a further, independent search of the handbook's own text (a
   full-text search for "January 1, 2001" across the entire handbook surfaced only the "Reserved"
   label and unrelated Board of Governors provisions, not an adoption date for the RPC). Section
   1 now states only that the Supreme Court of Georgia adopted the current rules, without a date.

## Gap list

1. No confirmation, in either direction, of whether a Georgia lawyer must notify a client of
   the right to fee arbitration before suing the client to collect fees. This was searched
   specifically because the brief for this pack asked about it. No such requirement appears in
   the Part VI rule text read directly (Rules 6-101 through 6-423); the only related text,
   Comment [9] to Rule 1.5, uses aspirational "should" language, not a mandatory duty. Search
   stopped there; the pack does not assert the requirement exists or does not exist beyond
   noting it was not found.
2. No Georgia-specific statutory contingent-fee cap or sliding scale (for example, a
   workers' compensation cap) was found. Search was limited to what surfaced through the rules
   handbook and general search; no state-agency schedule was located or read. Not included in
   the pack.
3. No deadline for filing a State Bar of Georgia grievance or complaint was located in the
   disciplinary rules read for this pack, and no Georgia case addressing whether laches applies
   to attorney discipline was found. Search stopped at the disciplinary procedure rules already
   read in the handbook; a further search across Georgia case law was not conducted, because
   the kit's scope does not extend to litigation strategy.
4. Magistrate court filing fee was not located as a figure, and no filing fee is stated anywhere
   in Part VI of the Bar's rules for a fee arbitration petition either. The magistrate court fee
   is understood to vary by county; the pack points the reader to the local clerk rather than
   stating a number. No county-level fee schedule was checked, because doing so for all 159
   counties was out of scope.
5. The exact text of the Supreme Court of Georgia's order adopting the current Rules of
   Professional Conduct, and its effective date, was not located or read after a further,
   independent search (see "Cut from the pack" above). The claim was removed from Section 1
   rather than left flagged, because the only support was a search synthesis and an indirect
   naming convention, not a dated document stating the date.
6. A direct, working fetch of the Official Code of Georgia Annotated at legis.ga.gov was
   attempted repeatedly and by several methods: the bare domain, a statutes landing page, and a
   guessed API document endpoint (HTTP 500) all returned nothing usable; the legislation search
   page and the codes landing page returned only a small JavaScript application shell (1,492
   bytes) with no rule text; a Wayback Machine (web.archive.org) CDX search for archived
   legis.ga.gov API endpoints found only one archived path (`/api/georgia-code/titles`, from
   2021-04-22) with no deeper section-level snapshots, and the live version of that same
   endpoint now returns HTTP 401 (requires authentication); a LexisNexis "Public Access" link
   advertised as Georgia's official free-access OCGA portal turned out to be a tracking-pixel
   page, not a searchable code database. Given these dead ends, this pack instead reached OCGA
   §§ 9-3-24, 9-3-25, and 15-10-2 through official Georgia Court of Appeals opinions that quote
   or cite them directly (see the confirmed table), which this pack treats as a valid primary
   source under the state_template.md source ranking (a dated official court publication). OCGA
   § 15-10-49 could not be reached this way and remains flagged. No human-verification challenge
   was encountered or attempted to be bypassed at any point in this research.
7. Whether a Rules of Professional Conduct violation is admissible as evidence of the standard
   of care in a Georgia legal malpractice case was not researched. The kit does not cover
   malpractice strategy, so this was not pursued past the limitation-period deadline needed to
   tell a reader a clock exists.
8. No dollar figure for what any particular remedy typically recovers, for either fee
   arbitration or the Clients' Security Fund. No verified dataset behind such a number exists,
   and none was sought.
9. Whether the magistrate court's jurisdictional limit, the "no jury trials" rule, or the
   petition-for-review appeal procedure vary by county, and whether lawyers may appear or a
   corporation must be represented by counsel in Georgia magistrate court, was not confirmed
   against an official source in the time available for this pass. The pack does not state
   anything about county-level variation, lawyer appearance, or corporate representation in
   magistrate court; a reader should confirm these with the local clerk.

## How to extend this file

Any new rule, deadline, dollar amount, phone number, or program name added to
[state_ga.md](state_ga.md) gets a row in one of the tables above, with its source and
retrieval date, in the same commit that adds it. A claim without a row is a defect.
