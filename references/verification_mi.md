## Flagged: sourced but resting on less than a primary or official publication

No claim in this pack rests on less than a primary or official publication. Every claim that
could not be sourced to the Michigan Supreme Court's own rule publications, the Michigan
Legislature's MCL pages, the AGC's or State Bar of Michigan's own official pages, or a Michigan
court rule read directly, was omitted and moved to the gap list instead, rather than sourced to
a lower tier and flagged.

## Gap list

1. The specific Michigan Supreme Court administrative order number and effective date under
   which the MRPC was first adopted was not confirmed. The MRPC's own Rule 1.0 comment
   describes the adoption process in general terms but does not cite an order number, and no
   separate confirmation was located. Search stopped there; the pack does not state an original
   adoption date.
2. Whether the State Bar of Michigan runs a distinct "client assistance" or "consumer
   assistance" intake function, separate from the AGC's request-for-investigation process, that
   will contact a lawyer on a client's behalf specifically over a fee or communication problem,
   was not confirmed. The State Bar's Client Protection Fund and general public-resources pages
   were reviewed and did not describe such a function; the AGC's own site was reviewed and
   describes only the request-for-investigation process. The pack does not state that Michigan
   has this kind of split intake.
3. The current small claims division filing fee was not confirmed against a specific statewide
   figure. Michigan district court filing fees are set under MCL 600.8371 and related sections,
   which were not independently fetched and read during this research. The pack tells the
   reader to confirm the fee with the specific district court instead of stating a number.
4. A State Bar of Michigan webpage describing the MCR 9.130 fee arbitration program in its own
   words, previously located during earlier research in this project, could not be relocated
   during this session. Several candidate URLs on michbar.org either 404'd, redirected to
   unrelated pages, or returned a client-rendered page shell with no extractable text. The pack
   does not attribute any quoted description of the program to the State Bar; every claim about
   MCR 9.130 in the pack is sourced only to the rule's own text.
5. Whether a Michigan lawyer must notify a client of the MCR 9.130 arbitration option before
   suing the client for fees was not found stated in MCR 9.130 or elsewhere in Chapter 9. This
   is treated as a confirmed negative finding within the rules reviewed, not a guarantee that no
   such requirement exists anywhere in Michigan law.
6. Whether a Michigan Rules of Professional Conduct violation is admissible as evidence of the
   standard of care in a Michigan legal malpractice case, beyond the general evidence-law
   deferral in MRPC 1.0(b), was not researched. This kit does not cover malpractice strategy, so
   research stopped at the limitation periods needed to tell a reader a clock exists.
7. Whether any Michigan-specific writing requirement exists for a non-contingent fee agreement
   above a dollar threshold, beyond MRPC 1.5(b)'s "preferably in writing" standard, was not
   found. The pack does not state a dollar-threshold writing requirement because none was
   located in the MRPC.
8. Local Michigan bar association fee-dispute committees, separate from the statewide MCR
   9.130 mechanism, were not confirmed to exist. Cursory checks of the Oakland County Bar
   Association, the Detroit Metropolitan Bar Association, and the Grand Rapids Bar Association
   did not turn up a dedicated attorney-client fee-dispute program comparable to what some
   other states run at the local level. This was not an exhaustive county-by-county search, and
   the pack does not claim that no local program exists anywhere in Michigan, only that MCR
   9.130 is the confirmed statewide mechanism.
9. Whether Client Protection Fund awards are paid in full or reduced at the Fund's discretion in
   ordinary cases (as opposed to the confirmed pro rata reduction when aggregate claims against
   one attorney exceed $375,000) was not confirmed. Search stopped at the Fund's own FAQ page,
   which did not address this beyond the pro rata provision already in the confirmed table.

Item 3 in an earlier draft of this list, whether the Client Protection Fund requires a
disciplinary complaint on file before a claim can proceed, is resolved and removed: the Fund's
own FAQ page ties completion of the AGC's or Attorney Discipline Board's decision on a filed
request for investigation to Standing Committee review of the claim. See the confirmed table.

## How to extend this file

Any new rule, deadline, dollar amount, phone number, or program name added to
[state_mi.md](state_mi.md) gets a row in one of the tables above, with its source and retrieval
date, in the same commit that adds it. A claim without a row is a defect.
