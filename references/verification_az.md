# Verification: Arizona

a primary or official publication. Flagged means the claim is real but rests on something less,
a live undated page, a secondary characterization, or a commercial mirror, and the flag says
why. The gap list is what was searched for and could not be sourced, and was therefore left out
of the pack. All URLs below were fetched on 2026-08-20.

## Gap list

1. No filing fee for the Fee Arbitration Program could be confirmed one way or the other beyond
   the overview page's description of the program as "free." Searched the current Rules of
   Arbitration of Fee Disputes (Rev. 10/21/2022) and the Basic Steps guide (filename dated 2023,
   no internal revision date found); neither names a filing fee. The pack states the program is
   free and stops there.
2. No requirement was found that a lawyer must notify a client of the right to Fee Arbitration
   before suing the client for unpaid fees. Searched the Program's own rules and ER 1.5; found
   nothing addressing pre-suit notice. Cut from any affirmative claim in the pack; stated as a
   negative finding instead.
3. No deadline for filing a State Bar disciplinary complaint was found. Searched the Discipline
   Process page and the Before Submitting a Charge page; neither states a filing deadline.
   Search stopped there; the pack states this as a negative finding rather than asserting no
   deadline exists.
4. Whether Arizona lets a plaintiff elect between a tort and a contract framing for a legal
   malpractice claim, with different limitation periods attached, was not resolved. *DMB Realco*
   and the statutes it applies treat legal malpractice as a single claim under the two-year
   period in § 12-542 regardless of framing, but no case squarely deciding an election question
   was located. Search stopped after reading *DMB Realco*; a full search of Arizona malpractice
   case law was outside this session's scope.
5. County-level small claims filing fee variation was not enumerated. The Arizona Supreme
   Court's statewide schedule gives a $30.00 base fee, but individual county surcharges were not
   searched county by county. The pack points the reader to their county's justice court clerk.
6. The full current text of ER 1.4, ER 1.15, and ER 1.16 was not independently fetched and read
   this session; only their existence and numbering were confirmed, from the ER 1.5 page's own
   navigation menu. The pack does not make any substantive claim about their content beyond the
   numbers, so no further sourcing was pursued.
7. An effective date for Rule 42 as a whole, separate from ER 1.5's own January 1, 2021
   effective date, was not located. The pack does not claim one.
8. The opinion text of *Elliott v. Videan* (1989) was not independently read. Its citation and
   date were confirmed via CourtListener's docket metadata, but the opinion page itself returned
   an anti-bot challenge on four attempts, casetext.com is blocked entirely, Leagle.com shows
   only the caption before a paywall, and law.justia.com returned no result for this case across
   three attempts. The pack's holding statement rests on the flagged Arizona Attorney Magazine
   characterization instead.
9. Whether a newer Rules of Arbitration revision exists after 10/21/2022 was not confirmed. The
   State Bar's public-facing fee arbitration page, <https://www.azbar.org/for-the-public/bar-provided-services-for-consumers/fee-arbitration/>,
   links only to the 10/21/2022 PDF; no azbar.org site search was available to check for a later
   one. The pack treats 10/21/2022 as current because it is the newest version found and it
   matches the dollar figure on both live State Bar pages.

## How to extend this file

Add a row to the confirmed table only after fetching the source directly in the same session
and reading the actual claim off the page, not off a search result summary. Add a row to the
flagged table when the best available source is undated, secondary, or a commercial mirror, and
say why. Add an item to the gap list, rather than a claim to the pack, whenever a search for a
figure or a deadline comes up empty; never fill the gap with a plausible-sounding number.
