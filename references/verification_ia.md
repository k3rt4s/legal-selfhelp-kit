## Gap list

1. Looked for a published time limit on filing a complaint with the Attorney Disciplinary Board. Checked the Board's own consumer brochure (Ethics_Brochure_Feb_2019) in full and the Board's complaint page on iowacourts.gov. Neither states a filing deadline. Chapter 34 and chapter 35 of the Iowa Court Rules were also checked for a limitations provision on filing a grievance and none was found in the portions read. This is stated in the pack as unconfirmed rather than asserting there is no deadline.

2. Looked for a filing deadline running from the date of loss for a Client Security Commission fund claim, and for whether Iowa requires a disciplinary complaint to be on file first as a precondition to a fund claim. Checked chapter 39 and chapter 40 of the Iowa Court Rules in full and the Commission's own page. Neither states a deadline tied to the date of loss or a discovery rule for fund claims, and neither states a disciplinary-complaint precondition. This is stated in the pack as unconfirmed.

3. Looked for whether Iowa Code section 631.1's small claims dollar limit or filing procedure varies by county. The statute itself, as read in full, sets a single statewide figure with no county-level variance language. No county-by-county comparison of local procedures was attempted beyond the statute's own text. The pack states this was not confirmed rather than asserting statewide uniformity in practice.

4. Looked for whether a corporate party in Iowa small claims court must appear through counsel specifically, as distinct from an individual's right to appear pro se or through an attorney under section 631.5. Section 631.5 as read addresses only the "defendant may appear in person or by attorney" language; it does not separately address corporate representation. This was not resolved further and is not stated as confirmed either way in the pack.

5. Looked for whether the Iowa State Bar Association's Fee Arbitration Service, referenced only by phone number in section 3, has published rules, a dollar floor or ceiling, a filing fee, or a deadline of its own, comparable to what was found for Polk County. The Iowa State Bar Association's public "How Much Do Lawyers Charge?" page (the one page found through search that seemed likely to cover this) returned an HTTP 200 with 40,581 bytes but no usable body text on inspection: the extracted content was Datadog analytics and logging JavaScript with zero occurrences of "arbitrat" anywhere in it, a client-side-rendered shell rather than real article content. No other iowabar.org page describing this specific service's mechanics was located within the scope of this task. This is recorded as a flagged referral rather than a confirmed program, and no mechanics for the ISBA service beyond its phone number are stated in the pack.

6. Looked for whether Iowa's Grievance Commission, as distinct from the Attorney Disciplinary Board, has its own restitution-recommending authority as part of a formal disciplinary sanction, separate from the fund-repayment condition found in Rule 34.25(1)(e) and Rule 34.27(2)(e). Chapter 36 (Grievance Commission Rules of Procedure) was fetched and partially reviewed, but a specific restitution-as-sanction provision distinct from the Board's reinstatement-condition rules was not confirmed within the scope of this task. The pack describes only the Rule 34.25(1)(e)/34.27(2)(e) fund-repayment pathway, which was confirmed directly, and does not assert a second, independent Grievance Commission restitution power.

7. Did not independently confirm whether other Iowa county bar associations beyond Polk County run fee arbitration committees, and if so, on what terms. This kit's search was limited to what a general web search and the Iowa judicial branch's own referral language surfaced; no systematic county-by-county check was performed.
