# Verification: Alabama (state_al.md)

Every claim in state_al.md traced to its source below. Retrieved 2026-08-20 unless noted.

All PDF and HTML sources below were fetched directly with curl.exe using a browser user
agent (and a referer header where a site required one), then converted to plain text with a
scratch Python script (pypdf for PDFs, a regex/html.unescape strip for HTML) and read in
full or grepped for the specific claim text, not accepted on HTTP status alone. The current
Alabama Legislature code site (alison.legislature.state.al.us) returns an identical
JavaScript-shell page of near-constant byte size for every distinct statute section
queried; this was confirmed by comparing content-length across several different section
URLs before deciding not to rely on it. Statutory claims instead cite dated Wayback Machine
snapshots of the legacy, now DNS-unreachable, alisondb.legislature.state.al.us pages, found
through the Wayback CDX API and fetched at their recorded snapshot timestamp.

## Gap list

1. Looked for the original Alabama Supreme Court order and effective date that first adopted the current Alabama Rules of Professional Conduct (the commonly cited January 1, 1991 date). Checked judicial.alabama.gov's rules index and the individual rule PDFs for Rules 1.4, 1.5, 1.15, 1.16, and the Preamble; none carry a footer or note with the original adoption order. A web search surfaced only secondary sources repeating the 1991 date. Search stopped there; the pack states the adopting mechanism (Alabama Supreme Court order) and cites a confirmed, dated example of that mechanism in action (the May 13, 2025 order), but does not state the original 1991 date.
2. Looked for any Alabama county bar association beyond Birmingham and Mobile that runs its own fee arbitration or fee dispute committee. Did not run a county-by-county search of all Alabama local bar associations; only followed up on the two that surfaced from a general search for Alabama fee dispute committees. The pack states plainly that its list of two is very likely incomplete.
3. Looked for whether Alabama Legal Services Liability Act Section 6-5-573's "only one form and cause of action" language displaces the six-year period in Ala. Code Section 6-2-34(8) for a fee-refund or unpaid-balance claim against a lawyer that is not framed as a breach of the standard of care. Read the full text of Sections 6-5-572, 6-5-573, 6-5-574, and 6-2-34 directly. Did not find an Alabama appellate decision or a state publication resolving this overlap, and case-holding research was out of scope for this task (CourtListener's opinion-text endpoint was not used for this reason, consistent with this project's usual finding that it returns unusable results). The pack states the ambiguity directly rather than picking one period, and tells the reader to get the applicable deadline confirmed by a lawyer.
4. Looked for a live online complaint-submission portal for filing a bar complaint against an Alabama lawyer, separate from the Office of General Counsel's disciplinary division page and its mailing address. Fetched and read the disciplinary division page and the ethics division page. Neither links to a distinct online complaint-submission form or portal. The pack points to the disciplinary division page itself as the correct starting point rather than asserting a portal that could not be confirmed.
5. Looked for a stated filing deadline for a disciplinary complaint against an Alabama lawyer. Checked Rules of Disciplinary Procedure 7, 9, 12, 13, 17, 21, 22, and 23, all fetched directly and grepped for limitation and deadline language. No stated deadline was found in any of them, and no rule was found stating affirmatively that no deadline applies. The pack records this as an open question rather than asserting either a deadline or its absence.
6. Looked for Alabama small claims filing fees. The Alabama Rules of Small Claims Procedure and the small claims guide PDF from judicial.alabama.gov do not state a statewide fee figure; fees are set at the county clerk level. Did not survey individual county clerks. The pack tells the reader to check with the filing county's clerk instead of stating a figure.
7. Looked at Alabama Rules of Professional Conduct Rule 1.4 (Communication) and Rule 1.15 (Safekeeping Property) for any deviation from the model rule bearing on fee reasonableness specifically, since section 2 of the pack is scoped to the reasonableness factors under Rule 1.5. Rule 1.4 as fetched is a shorter, two-subsection rule that does not carry the model rule's five enumerated communication duties; Rule 1.15 largely tracks the model's trust-accounting structure with Alabama-specific IOLTA definitions. Neither difference was included in the pack because section 2 is scoped to reasonableness factors, not the full communication or safekeeping rules; this is recorded here in case a future editor extends the pack's scope.

8. Looked for a fraud-concealment exception to the four-year outer cap in Ala. Code Section 6-5-574(a). An earlier draft of this pack asserted one. The full text of Section 6-5-574 was fetched from the Wayback snapshot cited above and searched; the word "fraud" does not appear in it, and the only exception written into the cap is for acts, omissions, or failures occurring before August 1, 1987. Subsection (b) makes the limitation period subject to the general computation statutes, which include Section 6-2-3 on fraud discovery, so an argument may exist by cross-reference, but that is an inference and not something the statute states. The assertion was cut from the pack rather than kept, and the pack now tells the reader not to assume such an exception exists.
