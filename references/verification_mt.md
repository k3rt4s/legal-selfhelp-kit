## Gap list

1. No separate statutory contingent fee percentage cap or sliding scale outside the MRPC was
   found. The MRPC text and MCA Title 37 chapter 61 (attorneys at law) were read; no dollar cap
   or sliding scale for contingent fees was located. Search stopped at those two sources.
2. No free itemized-accounting right comparable to some other states' rules was found in the
   MRPC. Rule 1.4 (communication) and Rule 1.15 (safekeeping property) were read in full; neither
   creates an express, standalone right to a free written accounting on request. Search stopped
   there; a broader search of MCA Title 37 was not conducted for this specific point.
3. No fee-arbitration-specific filing deadline, separate from the general contract statute of
   limitations, was found in the Rules on Arbitration of Fee Disputes. The full rules text was
   read. This is stated in the pack as an absence, not asserted as "no deadline exists at all."
4. The general per-defendant "appearance" fee is listed at $60 in the current MCA text
   (25-1-201(1)(b)) but at $70 in the district court's own Fee Schedule-Civil PDF (updated May
   2024). Both are official sources; they disagree, and the more recent dated source (the fee
   schedule) shows the higher figure. This pack could not confirm whether this general
   appearance fee applies on top of, or is unrelated to, the dedicated $10 small claims appeal
   line item, so neither figure was included in the true appeal cost stated in section 6. A
   reader should confirm the total cost directly with the district court clerk before filing an
   appeal.
5. No statutory statement was found on whether a Montana legal malpractice claim sounds in
   tort, contract, or either at the plaintiff's election. MCA 27-2-206 was read in full; it
   applies to "an action" against the lawyer without naming a theory. Montana case law on this
   point was not researched, consistent with this kit's scope excluding malpractice strategy.
6. No requirement that a disciplinary complaint be on file before an LFCP claim was found in the
   LFCP program page or the LFCP Rules PDF read for this pack. Both sources were read in full.
   This is stated as an absence, not a confirmed statement that no such requirement exists. (The
   LFCP Rules PDF does set a 3-year filing deadline from discovery of the dishonest conduct, under
   Rule 8C; that deadline is confirmed above and stated in the pack, and is not part of this gap.)
7. This pack did not independently verify, through Montana case law, how Montana courts actually
   apply a Rule of Professional Conduct violation as evidence of the standard of care in a
   malpractice case. That is a malpractice-strategy question outside this kit's scope.
8. Two documents fetched during research, the State Bar of Montana's "Disputing Attorney Fees"
   consumer page and its "Problems With a Lawyer" page, returned empty or unusable extracted
   text on the pages saved during this research session and were not used as sources for any
   claim in the pack. No claim in the pack rests on either page.
