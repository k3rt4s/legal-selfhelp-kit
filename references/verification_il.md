## Gap list

What was looked for and could not be sourced to a primary or official publication, and so was cut
from the pack:

- **Whether either side keeps a right to a trial after a CBA arbitration award.** The CBA's page
  confirms the award is binding and enforceable in court but does not address whether a losing
  party retains any right to challenge it afterward. Search stopped at the CBA's own page; no
  separate CBA rules document for the Committee on Professional Fees was located. (Whether the
  award itself is binding is now confirmed and is no longer a gap; see the confirmed table.)
- **Whether an Illinois lawyer must notify a client of any right to fee arbitration before suing
  for fees.** No such requirement was found in Rule 1.5, in ARDC materials, or in the CBA or DuPage
  program pages reviewed. Treated as a negative finding, not stated as a confirmed absence across
  all of Illinois law.
- **Local fee-dispute programs beyond Chicago and DuPage.** Lake County and Will County bar
  association sites were checked; neither surfaced a fee-dispute-specific program page. This does
  not mean no such program exists, only that none was confirmed. The pack states the list may be
  incomplete rather than naming these counties as having no program.
- **Illinois small claims filing fee.** A Cook County figure was found only through an unread,
  unconfirmed Cook County Clerk PDF filename during an earlier web search; that PDF was never
  directly fetched and read, so no figure is stated in the pack. Search stopped before confirming
  any county's fee against the county clerk's own current schedule; the pack instead points the
  reader to the circuit clerk.
- **Whether ARDC's disciplinary-restitution power (the "File a Complaint" page's own language) is
  grounded in a specific, separately numbered Supreme Court Rule, the way Texas's Rules 2.19 and
  3.11 are.** The ARDC page states the outcome in plain language but does not cite a rule number
  for it, and no such standing rule number was located elsewhere in the CPP Rules or the RPC.
  Described in the pack as a case-by-case outcome within a disciplinary proceeding rather than as
  a rule with its own citation, because no citable rule number could be confirmed.
- **A general 1998 Illinois Bar Journal guide to "mandatory arbitration in Illinois" was checked
  and found to concern general civil court-annexed arbitration under Supreme Court Rules 86-95
  (claims up to $50,000), not attorney fee dispute arbitration.** Confirmed by a full-text search
  for "fee arbitration" and "fee dispute" within the document (zero matches) and by reading its
  opening section. This source is not cited anywhere in the pack.
