## Gap list

1. No filing fee amount for the Mandatory Fee Arbitration Program was located. Business and
   Professions Code section 6200(a) allows the Board of Trustees to set a fee by rule but does
   not state a figure, and search stopped there rather than tracking down each local bar
   program's own fee schedule; the pack tells the reader to check with their local program.
2. Whether Code of Civil Procedure section 340.6's malpractice period applies identically
   regardless of whether the claim is pled in tort or in contract was not confirmed against case
   law. The statute's own text does not distinguish by theory, and the pack says only that much;
   no case law search was done because the kit does not cover malpractice strategy beyond
   flagging that a clock exists.
3. No statewide flat filing fee figure for small claims court was located. Search stopped at the
   jurisdictional statutes (Code Civ. Proc. sections 116.220, 116.221), which do not themselves
   list fees; the pack points the reader to the county clerk instead of stating a number.
4. No claim about a Rules of Professional Conduct violation creating a private cause of action.
   Rule 1.0(b)(3) states plainly that it does not, and the pack quotes that rule directly rather
   than relying on a case-law search beyond the two cases the rule's own comment already cites.
5. No dollar figure for what any particular remedy typically recovers. There is no verified
   dataset behind such a number, and none was sought.
6. Whether any county's local Mandatory Fee Arbitration program sets its own dispute-size floor
   or ceiling, separate from the statewide statute, was not researched. The pack states the
   statute has no ceiling or floor and does not survey individual county bar program rules,
   which was out of scope for a statewide pack; a reader should confirm with their county
   program directly.
7. Whether every county's small claims court charges the same filing fee tiers, and whether any
   county-level local rule affects the attorney-representation exceptions in Code of Civil
   Procedure section 116.530, was not researched beyond the statute's own text.
