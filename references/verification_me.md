## Gap list

1. Whether Maine recognizes an attorney's retaining lien over a client's file for unpaid fees.
   The independent verification pass found and read Ethics Opinion #51 (Dec. 5, 1984, reaffirmed
   as an Enduring Ethics Opinion in May 2017) and Ethics Opinion #120 (Dec. 11, 1991, reaffirmed
   April 2022), both of which hold that a lawyer may not condition return of a client's file on
   payment of fees or costs, and that the lawyer's remedy for unpaid fees is a civil suit, not
   withholding the file. This is current, twice-reaffirmed Board of Overseers guidance, and the
   pack and this file now cite it. It is still opinion-level authority, not a rule provision or a
   Law Court holding, since no Maine case addressing a retaining lien on a client's own file was
   found in this pass. A future verifier could still look for a reported Maine case on point.
2. RESOLVED. Whether the $300.00 "Jury Trial Fee in Superior Court" in Administrative Order
   JB-05-26 applies to a small claims jury trial de novo appeal. It does. MRSCP Rule 11 and the
   fee order are both silent on the connection, which is why two passes could not confirm it
   from those two documents alone. The Judicial Branch's own consumer guide, "A Guide to Small
   Claims Cases," PUB-006, January 2026, closes it: it states that a defendant appealing on
   questions of fact may request a jury trial in Superior Court, that there is a fee for a jury
   trial, and it points the reader to Administrative Order JB-05-26 for the amount. The pack now
   states the $300.00 as part of the appeal cost for a defendant who demands a jury.
3. The exact cost of preparing and forwarding the record on appeal under MRSCP Rule 11(b)-(d).
   The rule requires the appellant to pay "the costs of forwarding to the Superior Court the
   record on appeal," which is not a fixed dollar figure in the rule or the fee order.
   Transcript costs specifically were not confirmed to a dollar figure for small claims appeals.
4. The precise process and timeline the Board's Central Intake Office follows once a complaint
   is forwarded to Bar Counsel or the Grievance Commission, beyond what Bar Rule 3 and Bar Rule
   9 state. I did not trace every procedural step from complaint to disposition.
5. Whether Maine case law allows a rule violation to be used as evidence of the standard of
   care in a legal malpractice case beyond what the MRPC Preamble itself states. The Preamble
   language is confirmed above; how Maine courts have actually applied it was not researched.
6. The Board of Overseers' own founding date (I saw "1978" on an undated Board web page) and
   the exact administrative history of the client protection fund before its 1997 rule-based
   effective date were not confirmed against a primary source and were left out of the pack.
7. Any additional Professional Ethics Commission opinions beyond #51, #120, and #187 that might
   bear on file retention, retaining liens, or fee disputes. I searched the Board's ethics
   opinion index by keyword and title but did not read every one of the more than 200 listed
   opinions.
8. Whether Maine's small claims filing fee waiver process (in forma pauperis, referenced in
   MRSCP Rule 2(b)) has any dollar thresholds or income tests; the rule text itself leaves this
   to the court's finding without stating a numeric standard.
9. Whether any Maine case has addressed the interaction between a lawyer's Rule 1.5(g)
   mandatory-arbitration duty and a lawyer's ability to still sue for fees when the client
   never requests arbitration. This pack describes what the rule text says; it does not trace
   how Maine courts have applied it in a reported decision.
