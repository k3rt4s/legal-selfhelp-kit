## Gap list

1. The precise current article or order number under which the TDRPC is promulgated, now that
   Article X of the State Bar Rules has been repealed, was not confirmed. The only confirmation
   located was a 2009 Texas Supreme Court miscellaneous docket order describing the TDRPC as
   "Section 9 of State Bar Rules," which does not by itself establish the current article
   structure, if any. Search stopped there rather than guess at a number.
2. A statutory percentage cap on contingent attorney fees in Texas workers' compensation matters,
   commonly cited elsewhere as Texas Labor Code Section 408.221, was not confirmed. The Texas
   Department of Insurance's workers' compensation pages were checked and did not state this
   figure, and statutes.capitol.texas.gov was unreachable for the reason stated in the flagged
   table above. The pack does not state this cap.
3. A filing fee for the Dallas Bar Association's or the San Antonio Bar Association's fee
   dispute program was not stated on either program's own page either way. Search stopped at
   those pages; neither was treated as confirmed free or confirmed to carry a fee.
4. Whether Client Security Fund awards are paid in full or at a discretionary percentage of the
   loss, and whether the fund publishes any per-claim or aggregate per-lawyer cap, was not
   confirmed. Two figures found during research for a cap traced to unrelated programs (the
   Texas Equal Access to Justice Program's IOLTA rules, and the Texas Real Estate Recovery Trust
   Account) and were rejected rather than used; see the confirmed table. No further figure was
   located on the fund's own official page.
5. Whether a Texas Rules of Professional Conduct violation is admissible as evidence of a
   standard of care in a Texas malpractice case was not researched. This kit does not cover
   malpractice strategy, so research stopped at the limitation periods needed to tell a reader a
   clock exists.
6. Whether the Texas Deceptive Trade Practices Act applies to a lawyer's professional services
   was not resolved. Willis v. Maverick notes the question without deciding it, and no later
   source resolving it was researched. The pack states this as unsettled rather than stating a
   DTPA limitation period.
7. Whether Texas has any statute of repose applicable to legal malpractice claims, separate from
   the two-year discovery-rule limitation period, was not confirmed either way beyond the absence
   of one in the sources reviewed. Search stopped once no repose provision surfaced in the
   Willis v. Maverick and Hughes v. Mahaney & Higgins opinions or the Civil Practice and Remedies
   Code sections reviewed.
8. A dedicated phone number for the Client Security Fund, separate from the general Grievance
   Information Helpline, (800) 932-1900, was not confirmed.

## How to extend this file

[state_tx.md](state_tx.md) gets a row in one of the tables above, with its source and retrieval
date, in the same commit that adds it. A claim without a row is a defect.
