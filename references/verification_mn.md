## Gap list

1. Looked for the specific mechanics of any Minnesota district or county bar association's fee arbitration program: dollar floor or ceiling, filing deadline, filing fee beyond "free" for Ramsey County, whether an award binds one or both sides, and whether a lawyer must notify a client of the right to arbitrate before suing for fees. Found and fetched the Ramsey County Bar Association's fee-dispute landing page at mnbars.org, which links to a "Rules and Procedures" document and a "Petition Form," but both linked documents appear to be served through a script-driven document viewer that this kit's curl-based retrieval could not resolve to readable text. No other Minnesota county's fee arbitration program page was located and fetched. This is why section 3 states these mechanics as unconfirmed rather than giving figures for them.
2. Looked for a published aggregate cap the Client Security Board may pay across multiple claims against the same lawyer, as distinct from the $150,000 per-claim cap. Checked the CSB's FAQ page and its own rules page (Rule 3.14 and the surrounding rule text). Found only the per-claim cap and a discretionary factor letting the Board consider "the total amount of losses caused by the dishonest conduct of any one lawyer" when deciding a claim, not a stated aggregate dollar limit. No aggregate cap figure was located. This is omitted from the pack; section 5 states the per-claim figure only and notes the discretionary factor.
3. Looked for whether Minnesota's disciplinary system, under RLPR Rule 15(b)'s "protection of clients" clause or any other rule, has ever been used or interpreted to order a lawyer to repay a client. Searched the text of all 30 RLPR rules for "restitution," "repay," and "reimburse," and found none used as a client remedy. Did not search Minnesota case law or LPRB opinions for an instance of Rule 15(b) being applied this way, which was outside the scope of the retrieval method used for this task. Section 4 states the rule text finding (no restitution mechanism located) and flags Rule 15(b)'s broader clause as theoretically possible but unconfirmed, rather than asserting flatly that it has never happened.
4. Looked for a Minnesota statute of repose specific to legal malpractice, meaning an outer deadline running regardless of discovery or damage. Minn. Stat. section 541.05's text, as fetched and read in full, contains no repose provision naming legal malpractice or professional services claims. No separate repose statute for malpractice was located elsewhere within the scope of this task. This is recorded as an absence in section 7 rather than asserted as proof no such provision exists anywhere in Minnesota law.
5. Did not independently fetch or read the full opinion text of Antone v. Mirviss, 720 N.W.2d 331 (Minn. 2006). CourtListener's opinion-text endpoint returned an authentication error (401) and its HTML case page returned an empty response (202) to this kit's retrieval method, consistent with the general finding that CourtListener case holdings stay flagged rather than confirmed for this kit. The "some damage" accrual characterization in section 7 rests on the case citation and name only; see the Flagged table above.
6. Did not confirm whether Minnesota's $20,000 (or $4,000 consumer-credit) conciliation court jurisdictional limits, or the section 357.022 filing fee, vary by county. Minn. Stat. section 491A.01 and section 491A.02, as fetched and read in full, state these as statewide figures with no county-variation language found in the statute text itself. No county-by-county fee schedule comparison was attempted. Section 6 notes this was not confirmed rather than asserting statewide uniformity beyond what the statute's own text supports.
7. Did not independently trace the statutory citation for a conciliation court party's right to remove a judgment for a trial de novo in district court. Section 6 states that a removal right exists based on general familiarity with Minnesota conciliation court practice reflected in the Wayback-archived FAQ page, but this kit did not fetch and read the specific statute or court rule establishing that right, so no citation is given for it in the pack and this claim does not appear in the Confirmed or Flagged tables above; treat the general removal-right statement in section 6 as unconfirmed against a specific citation.
8. The independent reviewer removed a sentence in an earlier draft naming a specific individual as OLPR/LPRB director and describing a 2026 leadership transition. That fact was unsourced to any dated announcement, is not a legal claim, and would date the pack the moment the office changes hands again. The pack now names the offices only, not who currently holds them; a reader can check current leadership at olpr.mncourts.gov or lprb.mncourts.gov if it matters to them.
9. Checked whether the Rules on Lawyers Professional Responsibility address fee arbitration at all, since section 3's voluntary-on-the-lawyer claim rests on a single 2011 Director's article reached only through Wayback. Fetched RLPR Rule 6 and Rule 15 directly and found no mention of fee arbitration in either. Fee arbitration is administered entirely by local bar associations outside the court rule scheme, so this kit found no rule text to confirm or contradict the article's claim. Section 3 now states this directly so a reader knows the claim rests on one dated secondary source, not on a rule.
