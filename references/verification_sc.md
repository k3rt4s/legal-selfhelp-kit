## Gap list

1. This kit did not find a single confirmed date for South Carolina's original, wholesale
   adoption of the ABA Model Rules of Professional Conduct as Rule 407, SCACR. Only
   specific later amendment dates (2012 for Rule 1.5, 2021 for the technology-related
   rules) were confirmed. The pack states the confirmed amendment dates and does not
   claim an adoption date.
2. This kit did not find a stated filing fee for the Resolution of Fee Disputes Board's
   own application process. The application form itself only asks about fees paid to the
   lawyer, not a fee owed to the Board. The pack states this as an open question for the
   reader to confirm directly with the Bar rather than asserting the process is free.
3. This kit did not find a South Carolina rule or ethics opinion confirming or denying
   whether a lawyer may hold a client's file over an unpaid bill (a retaining lien).
   Rule 1.16(d) allows retaining papers "to the extent permitted by other law" but this
   kit did not locate that other law. The pack flags this as open rather than asserting
   an answer either way.
4. This kit did not find a South Carolina rule requiring a lawyer to notify a client of
   the right to use the Resolution of Fee Disputes Board before suing the client for
   unpaid fees, a notice requirement that exists in some other states. The pack does not
   claim such a requirement exists in South Carolina.
5. This kit did not independently verify a stated deadline for filing a complaint with
   the Office of Disciplinary Counsel. If a time limit exists, it was not found in the
   pages fetched for this pack; the pack does not state one.
6. This kit confirmed no county-by-county variation in the magistrate's court $7,500
   jurisdictional limit, but did not exhaustively check every county's local
   supplementary rules for procedural differences (such as local form requirements),
   only the statewide jurisdictional statute and the Bar's own statewide guide.
