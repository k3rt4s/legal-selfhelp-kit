# Where a Tennessee client can take a legal fee or conduct problem

Each of these is a separate forum with a separate purpose. Picking the wrong one wastes months. Read the whole list before you file anything.

## Consumer Assistance Program, Board of Professional Responsibility

The Board of Professional Responsibility runs the Consumer Assistance Program, or CAP, as an intake point for the public. CAP describes itself as a clearinghouse: it identifies the problem and finds the resource that can help.

CAP can:

1. Give information about how the process works.
2. Contact your lawyer, including sending a letter of concern if the lawyer has not responded to you.
3. Informally mediate a dispute.
4. Refer the matter to a fee dispute committee, a lawyer referral service, or Disciplinary Counsel.

CAP will not give you legal advice, recommend a lawyer, or intervene in a pending court matter. It declines matters involving serious misconduct such as theft of client funds, which go to Disciplinary Counsel instead.

Useful specifics:

- If your lawyer does not respond to your written correspondence, CAP will follow up if the lawyer has not answered within ten days. Write first, then escalate.
- On fees, CAP directs clients to request an itemized bill and review the fee agreement before escalating.
- On firing a lawyer, CAP advises a written discharge letter that requests the file and any unearned fee refund.

The Board's public line is 1-800-486-5714, and the site is <https://www.tbpr.org>.

When you file, the form asks you to choose. A formal complaint is for alleged ethical violations of a serious nature. An informal request for assistance is for concerns that do not rise to that level. Choosing "formal complaint" for what is really a billing disagreement tends to produce a closed file, not a refund.

## Local fee dispute committees

Tennessee has no statewide attorney fee arbitration program. Fee dispute mediation is run by local bar associations and varies by county. Many counties do not offer it at all.

Programs exist in or around: Memphis, Knoxville, Chattanooga, Bristol, Kingsport, Montgomery County, and Washington County.

If your county is on that list, this is usually the cheapest and fastest path for a genuine disagreement about the size of a bill. If your county is not on that list, your realistic options are direct negotiation, then General Sessions court.

Confirm the current program, its rules, and whether both sides must agree to participate with the local bar association before you rely on it.

## Tennessee Lawyers' Fund for Client Protection

The Fund reimburses losses caused by the dishonest conduct of a Tennessee lawyer. It is not a fee dispute forum and it is not malpractice insurance.

What it covers:

1. Unearned fees, where the lawyer showed a pattern of behavior evidencing no good faith intention to perform the services, or committed a clear disciplinary violation involving dishonest conduct.
2. Certain losses on an "investment" that would not have happened but for the attorney-client relationship.

What it does not cover: fee disputes. The Board distinguishes a fee dispute from dishonest conduct by asking whether the lawyer did any work, whether the billing was reasonable, and whether there was actual dishonesty rather than a breach of contract.

Two practical points:

- A claimant must file a written complaint with the Board of Professional Responsibility, and a claim can be dismissed if that is not done within 60 days of notice, unless the lawyer has been permanently disbarred.
- There is no published fixed maximum award. Approved claims are paid at a percentage set at the Board's discretion based on the fund balance and budget, so a valid claim does not guarantee full reimbursement.

The Fund's line is (615) 741-3097 and the site is <https://tlfcp.tn.gov>.

## General Sessions court

For getting money back, General Sessions is Tennessee's small claims forum, designed so that a non-lawyer can appear without counsel.

The civil jurisdictional limit is $25,000 in most counties. In Anderson, Davidson, Hamilton, and Knox counties the limit is $15,000. Davidson County is Nashville, so many Middle Tennessee clients are working against the lower number.

Attorney fees, court costs, and discretionary costs are not counted when deciding whether a judgment exceeds the jurisdictional limit.

Confirm the current limit and filing fee with the clerk in the county where you plan to file. Limits are set by statute and change.

## Tennessee Lawyer Assistance Program

If the underlying problem appears to involve a lawyer's impairment, the Tennessee Lawyer Assistance Program exists at (877) 424-8527. It is a help resource for lawyers, not a remedy for clients, but knowing it exists explains why some conduct gets handled the way it does.

## Choosing among them

1. You want the bill reduced and your county has a fee dispute committee: start there, after a written itemization request.
2. You want money back and there is no local program: written demand, then General Sessions.
3. The lawyer took a retainer and did nothing, or the money is missing: Board complaint, and look at the Client Protection Fund.
4. You want the conduct on the record regardless of money: Board complaint, understanding that discipline is not a refund.
5. You want your file: written request under RPC 1.16(d), then CAP if it is ignored.

These are not exclusive. A Board complaint and a General Sessions suit can both exist. But each has its own clock and its own paperwork, and running them without a plan is how people lose track.
