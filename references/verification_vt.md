# Verification: Vermont

Retrieval method: primary fetches used curl with a Chrome user-agent string and a
Google referer header, following redirects, checked for a 200 status and the expected
content type before parsing. Guessed vermontjudiciary.org URLs that returned 404 were
abandoned in favor of crawling from the site's own navigation and index pages
(attorneys.html, home.html, and the Professional Responsibility Board page) and
extracting real links with a regex over the raw HTML, per the project's retrieval
guidance. PDFs were downloaded and parsed with pypdf; both PDF and HTML text were
normalized with whitespace collapsing and entity stripping before searching. Where a
heading (for example "Rule 1.5," "Sanctions," or "Fee Disputes") appeared once in a
table of contents near the top of a document and again in the body, the later,
body-text occurrence was used, found by enumerating every match offset rather than
taking the first. All dates below are the date of retrieval during this session,
August 20, 2026.

This file was independently re-verified against source as a second pass. That pass
found that Vermont's court system migrated its primary domain from
vermontjudiciary.org to vtcourts.gov after the original research: most old page URLs
301-redirect and still resolve, but several specific dated PDF filenames (the V.R.P.C.
compiled text, the VBA ethics-opinions index, and the Client Security Fund Rules
filename) do not survive the migration and now 404. The Source cells below were
updated with the current working URLs found by re-crawling the sites' live navigation
pages rather than guessing filenames. No factual claim changed as a result of this
pass; only citations were repaired.

## Gap list

1. Fee Dispute Committee dollar floor or ceiling. The Committee's own public page and
   its info/FAQ sheet were both fetched and read in full. Neither states a minimum or
   maximum dollar amount the Committee will hear. This is recorded as a confirmed
   absence in the sources checked, not confirmed to be true of the program overall,
   since an unpublished internal practice could exist that these documents do not
   state.

2. Fee Dispute Committee's own filing deadline, separate from the Vermont Arbitration
   Act's 30-day post-award vacate window. The same two sources as above were checked
   and neither states a deadline running from the bill, the payment, or the end of
   representation for bringing a dispute to the Committee in the first place. Recorded
   as a confirmed absence in the sources checked.

3. Whether a Vermont lawyer must notify a client of the right to use the Fee Dispute
   Committee before suing the client for unpaid fees. Not addressed in the Committee's
   public page, its info sheet, or the V.R.P.C. text reviewed. Not searched beyond
   these three sources; recorded as an open gap rather than a confirmed absence.

4. Whether Vermont courts admit a V.R.P.C. violation as evidence of the standard of
   care in a legal malpractice case. The Scope note (paragraph [20], confirmed above)
   was read in full and does not carry the ABA model's separate evidentiary sentence;
   it addresses only whether a violation creates a private cause of action, not
   whether a violation is admissible as evidence of the standard of care in a
   separately-filed malpractice suit. No Vermont case law or secondary source on this
   specific evidentiary question was fetched and read during this session. Recorded as
   a gap; the pack does not state an answer either way.

5. Whether a Vermont legal malpractice claim sounds in tort (12 V.S.A. section 512,
   3 years, statutory discovery rule), in contract (12 V.S.A. section 511, 6 years),
   or either at the client's election. Both statutes were fetched and read; neither
   states which applies to a legal malpractice claim specifically, and no Vermont
   Supreme Court decision resolving the question was independently fetched and read
   during this session. The pack states both periods and both citations but does not
   assert an answer to this question.

6. Any Vermont statute of repose specific to legal malpractice, distinct from the
   7-year repose in the medical malpractice statute (12 V.S.A. section 521). Sections
   511, 512, 518, 519, 521, and 522 of chapter 23 were fetched and read; no repose
   period specific to attorney malpractice was found in any of them. Recorded as a
   confirmed absence in the sections checked, not a confirmed absence for the entire
   Vermont Statutes Annotated, since chapter 23 was not the only chapter that could
   theoretically bear on this.

7. Fee forfeiture as a distinct remedy for a serious breach of fiduciary duty by a
   lawyer (recognized as a remedy in some states independent of a straight contract
   claim). Not researched during this session at all; not mentioned in the pack.

8. Current, single consolidated text of Administrative Order 9. As noted in the
   Flagged table above, only an archived compilation current through January 11, 2016
   was fetched and read for the sanctions/restitution provision, strengthened by three
   individually-fetched recent amendment orders that do not touch Rule 8. A paywalled
   LexisNexis Advance link, confirmed to be the Judiciary's own intended path to the
   current text, was the only other pointer found; it was not a fetchable, readable
   document on this pass either.

9. Whether any Vermont county or division applies a small claims dollar limit
   different from the statewide $10,000/$5,000 figures in 12 V.S.A. section 5531.
   The statute itself, which applies statewide with no county carve-out, was fetched
   and read in full; no county-specific variation is stated in it, and no separate
   local-rule source was checked. Recorded as a confirmed absence in the statute
   itself.

10. Whether the Client Security Fund's exclusions list (Rule 8.D of the Fund's rules)
    contains an exclusion naming fee disputes or unearned advances explicitly, beyond
    the narrow "Dishonest Conduct" definition already confirmed above. The Fund rules
    PDF was fetched and Rule 8.D(1) through (3) were read, covering exclusions for
    family/employees of the lawyer and losses covered by bonds or insurance; the
    pack relies on the narrow definition of "Dishonest Conduct" itself
    (misappropriation, embezzlement, defalcation, conversion) to support the statement
    that an ordinary fee dispute is not covered, rather than on a separate explicit
    fee-dispute exclusion clause, because the full exclusions list (D)(4) onward was
    not read to its end during this session.
