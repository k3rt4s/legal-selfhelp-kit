# Idaho

This pack is for someone in a dispute with their own lawyer over fees, billing,
communication, getting their file back, or an unearned advance payment. It does not cover
suing a lawyer for malpractice. Retrieval date for this pack is 2026-08-20.

Read [national_baseline.md](national_baseline.md) first. This file covers only what Idaho
does differently, plus the Idaho-specific programs and deadlines.

What a reader in Idaho most needs to know:

- Idaho has an extra rule the ABA model does not have: Rule 1.16A, "Client Files." It answers
  the file-return question directly. A lawyer can withhold your file for unpaid fees under an
  enforceable agreement, but not if withholding it would cause you imminent prejudice.
- Idaho runs a statewide fee arbitration program at no cost to either side, but it is
  consensual on both sides. If your lawyer will not agree to binding arbitration, the Bar
  still appoints a mediator to try to resolve the dispute, at no cost, but the outcome will
  not be binding.
- Idaho's small claims court bars attorneys from appearing at the hearing itself, for either
  side, with narrow exceptions.
- Idaho's legal malpractice clock runs from the act or omission, not from when you discovered
  it, and it is not extended by an ongoing relationship with the lawyer.

## 1. Rules of professional conduct, numbering and adoption

The Idaho Supreme Court adopts the Idaho Rules of Professional Conduct (IRPC) by court order.
The current version took effect July 1, 2004, replacing an earlier version that had been
effective since November 1, 1986. Cite a rule in a letter the way Idaho numbers it, for
example "IRPC 1.5" or "Idaho Rule of Professional Conduct 1.5." Idaho follows the standard
Model Rule numbering for the rules this kit covers: 1.4 (communication), 1.5 (fees), 1.15
(safekeeping property), and 1.16 (declining or terminating representation). A reader looking
for those numbers will find them in the same place as in the ABA model.

Idaho adds one rule the model does not have, inserted right after 1.16: Rule 1.16A, "Client
Files." It is covered in section 2 below because it is the state's biggest deviation and the
direct answer to the file-retention question.

The official full text is published by the Idaho State Bar at
https://isb.idaho.gov/wp-content/uploads/IRPC-acc.pdf. The Bar's landing page for the rules,
which states the adoption and effective dates, is https://isb.idaho.gov/bar-counsel/irpc/.

## 2. Deviations from the model reasonableness factors

Idaho Rule 1.5(a) lists the same eight reasonableness factors as the ABA model, in the same
order and substantially the same wording. No deviation was found there.

Two real deviations exist elsewhere in the rule, and they matter more than the factor list:

1. Rule 1.5(f): a client has an affirmative right, on reasonable request, to a free written
   accounting of fees and costs claimed or already collected. The model rule has no equivalent
   provision. The accounting must itemize hourly charges, costs, interest assessments, and
   past due balances, and must describe the services performed for hourly charges. If you
   think you were overbilled, you can ask for this accounting in writing and the lawyer must
   provide it without charge.
2. Rule 1.16A, "Client Files," an entire rule the ABA model does not have. It defines the
   client's file broadly (correspondence, filings, discovery materials, notes, and more), and
   it sets the rules for when a lawyer must hand it over, what a lawyer may withhold, and what
   a lawyer may charge for copies. The two points that matter most in a fee dispute:
   - Rule 1.16A(f): a lawyer is not required to turn over documents the client owes for under
     an enforceable fee agreement but has not paid for, unless withholding those documents
     would cause the client imminent prejudice. In plain terms, a lawyer can hold your file
     over unpaid fees, but only up to the point where doing so would actively hurt your case
     or your interests.
   - Rule 1.16A(e): a lawyer may charge the client for the actual cost of copying and postage,
     but not for the lawyer's own time and labor to assemble the file.
   Rule 1.16A(c) lists narrower categories a lawyer may always withhold regardless of payment,
   such as internal firm administrative communications and the lawyer's own consultations with
   malpractice counsel about the dispute.

No dollar-threshold writing requirement beyond what Rule 1.5(b) and 1.5(c) already require was
found. No separate statutory contingent fee percentage cap or sliding scale outside the IRPC
was found; this is listed in the gap list below rather than asserted as "none exists."

## 3. Attorney fee arbitration

Idaho runs one statewide fee arbitration program, administered by the Idaho State Bar under
Idaho Bar Commission Rules 700 through 708. The Bar's program description is at
https://isb.idaho.gov/bar-counsel/fee-arbitration/. The operative rules are published at
https://isb.idaho.gov/wp-content/uploads/SECTION-VII-Fee-Arbitration-acc.pdf.

- Who can start it: either the client or the lawyer can file a petition, on a Bar-approved
  form, for binding fee arbitration (Rule 703(a)).
- Consent, and what happens without it: binding arbitration requires both sides to consent.
  When one side files a petition, the Bar sends it to the other side and asks for consent to
  binding arbitration within 21 days (Rule 703(b)). If the other side does not consent in that
  window, the matter does not go to binding arbitration. But whenever a client has filed the
  petition, the Bar appoints a mediator to attempt non-binding mediation regardless of whether
  the other side ever consents to arbitration (Rule 703(b)). If mediation does not resolve it
  and the other side never consented, the rules provide no further Bar process, and both sides
  are left to their ordinary civil remedies (Rule 703(c)). In short: a client is guaranteed a
  free attempt at mediation, but not guaranteed a binding decision unless the lawyer agrees.
- Panel size: a single arbitrator hears disputes of $2,500 or less; a three-member panel hears
  disputes over $2,500 (Rule 704(a)). A three-member panel is two Bar members in good standing
  and one non-lawyer Idaho citizen (Rule 702(b)). This is a hearing-size rule, not an
  eligibility ceiling; there is no dollar cap on which disputes the program will hear.
- Cost: the rules do not provide for paying the mediators or arbitrators, and do not describe
  a filing fee (Rule 702(e), Rule 703). No fee to file was found in the rules.
- Deadline: the panel does not have jurisdiction over a dispute that is already beyond the
  applicable statute of limitations (Rule 702(a)(1)(B)), so file before your contract
  limitation period (see section 7) runs out. No separate, shorter fee-arbitration-specific
  filing window was found.
- Binding effect: where both sides consented, the decision binds them to the extent provided
  by Idaho's Uniform Arbitration Act, Idaho Code section 7-901 et seq., and can be enforced in
  court (Rule 706(a)). There is no notice requirement found in these rules that a lawyer must
  tell a client about the right to arbitrate before suing for fees.
- Undoing a binding award: Idaho does not give either side a further appeal to the Bar. A
  losing party can ask a court to vacate the award only on the narrow statutory grounds in
  Idaho Code section 7-912, such as the award being procured by fraud or corruption, or an
  arbitrator's misconduct or exceeding their powers. A motion to vacate must be made within 90
  days after the party receives a copy of the award. Text at
  https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/Title7/T7CH9/SECT7-912/.

## 4. Bar complaint process

The Idaho State Bar's Bar Counsel office handles attorney discipline. A client files a
grievance through the process described at https://isb.idaho.gov/bar-counsel/grievance-faq/.

- Idaho does not run a separate informal, consumer-assistance track apart from the formal
  grievance process. The Bar's own grievance FAQ page directs a pure fee dispute to the fee
  arbitration program described in section 3, not to a disciplinary grievance, because a
  grievance does not produce a refund.
- Idaho Bar Commission Rule 525(o) states directly that "proceedings under these Rules shall be
  exempt from all statutes of limitations," so there is no deadline to file a grievance.
- A grievance goes to Bar Counsel's staff, and can be referred to a review panel of the
  Professional Conduct Board. Filing a grievance can result in discipline against the lawyer.
  It does not by itself get you money back.
- Restitution: Idaho Bar Commission Rule 506(i) lists restitution as a form of discipline a
  hearing panel or the Professional Conduct Board may impose, and Rule 509(c)(3) confirms it
  can be imposed as a sanction. This is discretionary, decided case by case, and is not a
  guaranteed outcome of filing a grievance. Source:
  https://isb.idaho.gov/wp-content/uploads/SECTION-V-Rules-For-Review-of-Professional-Conduct-acc.pdf.
- The grievance FAQ page explicitly states the Bar does not have jurisdiction over legal
  malpractice claims; those go to court, not to Bar Counsel.

## 5. Client protection fund

Idaho's fund is the Client Assistance Fund, created under Idaho Code section 3-409 and
governed by Idaho Bar Commission Rules 600 through 616. The Bar's program page is
https://isb.idaho.gov/bar-counsel/client-assistance-fund/. The operative rules are at
https://isb.idaho.gov/wp-content/uploads/SECTION-VI-Client-Assistance-Fund-acc.pdf.

- What it covers: losses caused by a lawyer's "dishonest conduct," meaning theft, embezzlement,
  wrongful taking or conversion of money or property, or borrowing from a client without
  intent or reasonable ability to repay (Rule 601).
- The one point that matters most for this kit: Idaho's definition of dishonest conduct
  expressly includes "refusal to return unearned fees received in advance as required by Rule
  1.16" of the IRPC (Rule 601, as stated on the Bar's Client Assistance Fund page). So an
  unearned advance the lawyer will not refund can potentially be a covered claim here, not
  just a fee-arbitration matter. An ordinary disagreement over whether a fee was reasonable is
  not covered; the fund does not compensate malpractice, and the Bar's page cites a reported
  Idaho Supreme Court case on that point.
- Exclusions: losses covered by any bond, surety agreement, or insurance policy, to the extent
  covered; losses to close family members or employees of the lawyer causing the loss; and
  losses recoverable under a financial institution's blanket bond (Rule 601 and 604).
- Per-claim cap: $25,000 (Rule 606, as stated on the Bar's page and consistent with the rule
  text). No separate published annual or per-lawyer aggregate cap was found.
- Deadline: a claim must be filed no later than three years after the claimant knew or should
  have known of the lawyer's dishonest conduct (Rule 606).
- No requirement that a disciplinary grievance be filed first was found in the rules read.
- Process: after a claim is filed, the attorney is notified and given 21 days to respond, a
  hearing is held, a committee issues findings forwarded to the Board of Commissioners, either
  side may file exceptions, and either side may seek review by the Idaho Supreme Court (Rule
  605). Awards are discretionary; the rules describe a per-claim maximum but do not guarantee
  full payment of every claim.

## 6. Small claims or equivalent court

Idaho's court is the Small Claims Department of the Magistrate Division, created under Idaho
Code section 1-2301.

- Jurisdictional limit: claims for recovery of money, or personal property, up to $15,000 per
  claim. No punitive damages or pain-and-suffering damages are available in this court. Idaho
  Code 1-2301, current text at
  https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/Title1/T1CH23/SECT1-2301/.
- No county-by-county variation in that dollar limit was found; it is set by the statewide
  statute.
- Attorneys: no attorney may appear with or for a party at a small claims hearing, including
  pretrial and posttrial motions in that department (Idaho Code section 1-2307, and Idaho
  Rules for Small Claims Actions, Rule 8(b), which match each other). The exceptions: an
  attorney may appear in a post-judgment proceeding to execute the judgment (including
  examining the judgment debtor), and an attorney may appear as a party to their own case
  unless they got the claim by assignment. You can still have a lawyer help you prepare or
  advise you behind the scenes; the lawyer just cannot appear with you at the hearing itself.
  Idaho Rules for Small Claims Actions, Rule 8, at
  https://isc.idaho.gov/rules-procedure/irsca.
- Removal and appeal: a party dissatisfied with a small claims judgment can appeal, which
  results in a trial de novo before a magistrate acting in that capacity (a "lawyer
  magistrate," outside the small claims department itself). Because the appeal is no longer
  "in the small claims department," attorneys can appear on appeal. Idaho Code 1-2311
  (appeal procedure) and Idaho Rules for Small Claims Actions, Rule 15 (appeals).
- True cost of an appeal: the notice of appeal, and the filing fee, must be filed within 30
  days of the judgment (Idaho Code 1-2311; Idaho Rules for Small Claims Actions, Rule 15(b)).
  Idaho Code 1-2311 names a $20 statutory fee paid to the county treasurer, but that is only one
  component of what the clerk actually collects. The Idaho Supreme Court's own current civil
  filing fee schedule lists a separate line item, "Small Claims Dept to Magistrate," for this
  exact appeal, and the total collected is $81.00, once the other fund allocations the schedule
  requires (judges' retirement, county facility, county district court, and court technology
  funds) are added to the $20 base. Budget $81, not $20, for the filing fee itself. Idaho
  Supreme Court, Appendix A Filing Fee Schedule, Category L.1, at
  https://api.isc.idaho.gov/uploads/Rules_PDFs/1775633983169-Appendix-A-Civil-Case-Filing-Fees-071224.pdf.
  Beyond that filing fee, if you lose the appeal, you can be ordered to pay the other side's
  appeal costs, capped at $50 under Idaho Rules for Small Claims Actions, Rule 15(e). Rule 15(f)
  also lets the court award the winning side's attorney's fees under Idaho Code 12-120(6) if
  that side used a lawyer; read on its own, 12-120(6) is written for postjudgment fees spent
  collecting a judgment, not fees for the appeal itself, but Rule 15(f) is the operative,
  currently effective court rule and it expressly applies that statute to the appeal, so a
  losing appellant should still expect possible exposure to the other side's attorney's fees.
  No appeal bond requirement was found in either the statute or the small claims rules.
- Filing fee for the initial small claims case: the Idaho courts' civil filing fee schedule
  lists a small claims filing fee totaling $69.00 (a $26 base fee plus smaller add-on fees).
  Idaho Supreme Court, Appendix A Filing Fee Schedule, effective July 12, 2024, at
  https://api.isc.idaho.gov/uploads/Rules_PDFs/1775633983169-Appendix-A-Civil-Case-Filing-Fees-071224.pdf.
  Confirm the current fee before filing, since fee schedules are revised from time to time.
- No small-claims rule specific to suing a lawyer, beyond the general attorney-appearance
  restriction above, was found.

## 7. Statutes of limitation

- Written contract: 5 years. Idaho Code section 5-216, at
  https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/Title5/T5CH2/SECT5-216/. A written fee
  agreement dispute is usually governed by this period.
- Oral contract: 4 years. Idaho Code section 5-217, at
  https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/Title5/T5CH2/SECT5-217/.
- Legal malpractice: 2 years, and it is styled as an action against "a licensed professional"
  for wrongful act, neglect, or malpractice. Idaho Code section 5-219(4), at
  https://legislature.idaho.gov/statutesrules/idstat/Title5/T5CH2/SECT5-219/.
- Accrual rule: the 2-year period runs from the time of the occurrence, act, or omission
  giving rise to the claim, not from when the client discovered it. The statute expressly
  states the cause of action is deemed to have accrued as of the occurrence and is not tolled
  or extended by continuing consequences of that act or by a continuing professional
  relationship with the lawyer. A narrow discovery-based exception exists only where a foreign
  object was left in a person's body or where there was fraudulent concealment; in that narrow
  case the action can be brought within one year of discovery, or two years from the
  occurrence, whichever is later. This exception is written primarily for medical cases; do
  not assume it applies to an ordinary legal fee or communication dispute.
- Idaho Code 5-219(4) applies to professional malpractice regardless of whether the claim is
  framed in tort or contract; this pack did not find a separate, distinct limitation period
  for a malpractice claim framed purely in contract, and does not assert one.

This section exists so you know a clock is running and can get your own counsel in time. This
kit does not cover how to bring a malpractice claim.

## 8. Private right of action for a rules violation

The IRPC's own Scope section states, consistent with the ABA model, that a violation of a rule
should not itself give rise to a cause of action against a lawyer and should not create a
presumption that a legal duty was breached. It also states that because the rules do set
standards of lawyer conduct, a violation may be evidence of a breach of the applicable
standard of conduct in a case that is otherwise a recognized claim. Source: IRPC Scope
section, in the full text at https://isb.idaho.gov/wp-content/uploads/IRPC-acc.pdf.

This pack did not independently verify, through Idaho case law, how Idaho courts actually
apply a rule violation as evidence in a malpractice case; that is a malpractice-strategy
question outside this kit's scope, and is listed in the gap list below rather than asserted.

Your actual money routes in Idaho are: a contract claim on the fee agreement (section 7), the
fee arbitration program (section 3), and the Client Assistance Fund for dishonest conduct such
as an unrefunded unearned advance (section 5). Discipline, including the restitution mechanism
noted in section 4, is a separate track run by the Bar, not a lawsuit you control.

## Confirm before you rely on this

Before you act, confirm the current status of the two Rule 5-219 limitation periods and the
current small claims dollar limit directly against Idaho's official legislature site, since
statutes are amended from year to year. Also confirm the current fee arbitration and Client
Assistance Fund forms and any filing fees directly with the Idaho State Bar, since program
administration details change even when the underlying rule does not.
