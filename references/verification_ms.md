# Verification: Mississippi (state_ms.md)

Every claim in state_ms.md traced to its source below. Retrieved 2026-08-20 unless noted.
All courts.ms.gov and msbar.org fetches used curl.exe with a Chrome-style user agent and a
Google referer, with content type checked before treating a fetch as real, and the returned
body was grepped for the specific claim text before being treated as confirmed. No free
official source for the Mississippi Code itself could be reached this session; every
Mississippi Code citation in the pack is sourced to law.justia.com, a commercial mirror, and
is therefore Flagged rather than Confirmed, even where the text itself was directly fetched
and read.

## Gap list

1. Looked extensively for the Mississippi Clients' Security Fund's own governing rules: coverage terms, exclusions, per-claim cap, aggregate cap, and any filing deadline. The only publicly reachable statement is the Bar Bylaws' Article 9-11, which names the administering committee and points to "the resolution creating the same" without publishing that resolution anywhere this kit could find. Two guessed URL patterns for a dedicated fund page both returned HTTP 404. On independent re-verification, this kit additionally enumerated msbar.org's own sitemap XML files (sitemap_index.xml, page_sitemap.xml, and a document-library sitemap) and searched them for any fund-named page or document, and separately queried the Wayback Machine's CDX API for historical snapshots of msbar.org URLs containing "security" or "fund." The only results found were archived snapshots (2003, 2013, 2017) of a Bar committee-membership page describing the Committee's job as reviewing claims and recommending payouts "in accordance with the rules of procedure of the fund," never publishing those rules themselves at any point across all three snapshots. A phone call to the Bar was still not attempted, remaining outside this task's scope. Nothing about the fund's terms appears in the pack.
2. A WebSearch-generated summary falsely attributed specific Mississippi Clients' Security Fund dollar figures ($50,000 aggregate, $10,000 initial, $2,500 attorney-fee-claim cap) to corporate.findlaw.com. Direct fetch and full-text search of that exact URL found zero mentions of "Mississippi" anywhere in the page. This figure is not a real finding and does not appear in the pack. On independent re-verification, a second WebSearch-surfaced figure was checked and also rejected: a claim that Mississippi once capped total Clients' Security Fund payments at $5,000 per lawyer. Tracing that figure through its cited source found it originates from a 1995 Connecticut Office of Legislative Research report, itself citing a 1993 ABA survey, over thirty years old and from neither Mississippi nor the Mississippi Bar. Neither figure is used in the pack. Flagged here as a trap for a later pack: a WebSearch summary is not a source, and these are two concrete cases in the same search topic of a figure that reads as plausible but is either fabricated, misattributed, or too stale to use as current.
3. Looked for whether Mississippi courts have applied the Section 15-1-49(2) latent-injury-or-disease discovery rule to a legal malpractice claim specifically, as opposed to only physical injury/disease claims. This would require reading Mississippi case law, which was outside the primary/official-statute-and-rule scope of this task. The pack states the discovery rule's statutory text and flags its uncertain application to malpractice, rather than asserting it applies.
4. Looked for a Mississippi statute of repose specific to legal malpractice. None found in Title 15, Chapter 1; the only statute of repose located, in Section 15-1-36, applies only to medical malpractice. This is stated as a negative finding in the pack, not silently omitted.
5. RESOLVED on independent re-verification. Miss. Code Section 25-7-25(1)(a) sets a single statewide justice court civil filing fee, $25.00, with no county-level variation in the statute text, paid in advance under Section 9-11-10. The same statute, Section 25-7-25(1)(g), separately sets a $5.00 fee for a justice court appeal with proceedings and bond, on top of the appeal bond required under Section 11-51-85, a cost the original drafter's text did not surface. The pack now states both figures instead of directing the reader to the county clerk. One related question stays open, see item 11 below.
6. Looked for whether Mississippi requires a corporation or other business entity to be represented by counsel in justice court specifically, and separately for any statute or rule capping how many claims a person may file in justice court in a given year. The Rules of Justice Court were read in full and resolved neither question; this kit knows the corporate-representation requirement exists in other Mississippi courts but did not find or rule out a justice-court-specific provision, and found no per-year filing cap anywhere in the RJC text or Title 9/Title 25 statutes reviewed for this pack. The pack states both as unresolved rather than asserting an answer either way.
7. Looked for an effective/amendment date specific to Rule 1.5 of the Mississippi Rules of Professional Conduct, separate from the July 1, 1987 adoption date of the RPC as a whole. The current published RPC text carries amendment notes on some individual rules (for example Rule 1.16(d), amended effective July 1, 2011) but not on Rule 1.5, which carries no amendment note in the current text. The pack states the RPC's overall adoption date and notes that Rule 1.5 itself carries no separate amendment date, rather than inventing one.
8. Looked for a Mississippi RPC Scope-section paragraph numbering scheme comparable to Utah's bracketed paragraph [20]. The current published Mississippi RPC Scope section text was read in full and does not use bracketed paragraph numbers at all. The pack cites the Scope section by name only, not by a paragraph number, because none exists in the current text.
9. Did not attempt to independently verify the current Mississippi Bar Center street address or CAP's stated phone number and email through a second source; both were read directly from the msbar.org CAP page fetched this session and are treated as current based on that single official source.
10. Two Mississippi Code sections initially guessed as the justice-court appeal statute, Section 11-51-75 (local governing authority appeals) and Section 11-51-79 (county court appeals), were fetched and read in full and confirmed NOT to govern justice court civil appeals, before Section 11-51-85 was identified via WebSearch and independently confirmed by direct fetch. Flagged here as a trap for a later pack: adjacent-numbered statutes in the same chapter can cover a related but wrong proceeding, and only reading the fetched text (not the section title alone) caught this.
11. Looked for whether Miss. Code Section 25-7-13's circuit court civil docketing fee ($135.00 total) applies a second time when a justice court appeal transcript is docketed in circuit court for trial de novo under Rule 27 of the Rules of Justice Court, as opposed to being covered by the Section 11-51-85 appeal bond's own "costs accrued and likely to accrue" language. Section 25-7-13, Section 25-7-25, the Rules of Justice Court, and the Uniform Civil Rules of Circuit and County Court referenced by Rule 27 were checked and none resolves this either way. The pack tells the reader to budget for the bond and the $5.00 appeal fee at minimum and to ask the circuit clerk whether a docketing fee also applies, rather than asserting a total appeal cost.
