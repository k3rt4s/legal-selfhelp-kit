## Gap list

1. The exact scope of Rule 1.8(i), the DC RPC rule that governs a lawyer's lien on client
   property or funds. Rule 1.16 Comment [11] cross-references it and quotes two DC Court of
   Appeals decisions on retaining liens, and that cross-reference is what the pack relies on
   for section 2. I did not independently fetch and read Rule 1.8(i)'s own text in this pass, so
   the pack does not state the lien's exact conditions beyond what Comment [11] itself says.
2. The exact discovery-rule test DC courts apply to determine when a legal malpractice claim
   accrues under DC Code 12-301. I confirmed Knight v. Furlow, 553 A.2d 1232 (D.C. 1989), is a
   real, correctly cited case through the CourtListener search API, which returns only metadata
   and a short header snippet, not the opinion body. The full opinion text sits behind a
   WAF-protected endpoint I was instructed not to try to circumvent, and no other primary or
   official source for the opinion's holding was located in this pass. The pack states that a
   discovery-based rule exists and names the case, but does not state the rule's specific
   elements, and states no statute of repose because none was found in the statute's own text.
3. Whether a specific numbered DC Code subsection separately governs legal malpractice claims,
   as opposed to the general contract and catch-all provisions in 12-301(a)(7) and (a)(8). I
   read the full current text of section 12-301 and found no malpractice-specific subsection;
   this pack treats malpractice as falling under the general framework, consistent with the
   statute's own text, but did not trace how DC case law has classified malpractice claims for
   accrual purposes beyond the Knight v. Furlow citation in gap 2.
4. Whether the Clients' Security Trust Fund has ever adopted separate Trustee regulations
   setting an aggregate annual cap or a per-lawyer cap, beyond the $100,000 per-claim figure
   published on the Fund's own page. Rule XII, section 6, contemplates the Trustees adopting
   regulations subject to Board of Governors approval, but I did not locate any such regulations
   published where I could fetch and read them. The pack states that no such cap was found in
   the material reviewed, which is a confirmed absence in what was checked, not proof that no
   such regulation exists anywhere.
5. Whether DC's small claims filing fee has an income threshold for its fee-waiver process. The
   court's own Civil Filing Fees page confirms a Fee Waiver Application exists and must be filed
   at the same time as the complaint and summons if a party cannot afford the filing fee, but
   does not state an income threshold or other eligibility test for it. This was not researched
   further in this pass.
6. Whether any additional DC Bar Legal Ethics Opinions beyond Opinion 376 bear on the
   mandatory-arbitration-clause question, or on the retaining-lien question in section 2 beyond
   what Rule 1.16 Comment [11] itself states. The ethics opinion index was not exhaustively
   searched.
7. The precise procedural timeline ODC follows internally between assigning a preliminary
   inquiry and reaching a disposition, beyond the general description on ODC's own "What Should
   You Expect" page. Rule XI's full procedural detail on Hearing Committee practice was fetched
   but not exhaustively read line by line.
