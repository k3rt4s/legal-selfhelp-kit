## Gap list

1. Whether Columbus, Toledo, or Dayton bar associations run a client fee dispute program,
   and on what terms, was not researched. Search stopped after confirming Cleveland
   Metropolitan (mandatory once invoked, binding) and Cincinnati (voluntary both sides);
   time did not allow checking every county or city bar association in Ohio. The pack
   states the list is likely incomplete rather than naming a program that was not checked.
2. No statewide filing deadline for an Ohio disciplinary grievance was found in the
   sections of Gov.Bar.R. V reviewed (Sections 5, 9, and 10 on grievance committees,
   filing, investigation, and cooperation). The rule's full text runs well beyond these
   sections and was not read end to end, so this is stated as "none found in the sections
   reviewed," not as a confirmed absence of any deadline anywhere in the rule.
3. Whether a proven Rules of Professional Conduct violation is admissible as evidence of
   the standard of care in an Ohio legal malpractice case beyond what the rule's own Scope
   section states was not independently researched in Ohio case law. The kit does not
   cover malpractice strategy, so this was not pursued past the Scope section's own
   language, already covered in the confirmed table.
4. A statewide filing fee for small claims division was not located; fees are set at the
   county or municipal court level. The pack points the reader to the local clerk instead
   of stating a number.
5. Whether Ohio law recognizes fee forfeiture, or any other client remedy for a rules
   violation beyond contract, fee dispute resolution, and the Client Protection Fund, was
   not researched, consistent with this kit's scope.
6. Two documents fetched during research were confirmed to be from other states and
   excluded entirely: a PDF at osbar.org, which is the Oregon State Bar's fee dispute
   program, not Ohio's; and a PDF titled for the Rules for the Government of the Judiciary
   of Ohio (GOVJUD.pdf), which governs judicial conduct, not attorney discipline, and was
   not used for any claim in this pack.

Senate Bill 219's amendment to R.C. 2305.06, effective September 23, 2026, was researched
and resolved rather than left open: it only adds a cross-reference to R.C. 2305.041(B), an
oil-and-gas lease provision, and leaves the six-year contract period unchanged. See the
confirmed table.

## How to extend this file

Any new rule, deadline, dollar amount, phone number, or program name added to
[state_oh.md](state_oh.md) gets a row in one of the tables above, with its source and
retrieval date, in the same commit that adds it. A claim without a row is a defect.
