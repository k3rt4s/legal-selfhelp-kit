# Provable delivery

## Scope

This is about proving a letter arrived, not about legal service of process. If your matter
reaches a point where a summons, a complaint, or another court paper must be formally served,
that has its own state rules and its own required method. This file does not cover that. It
covers the letters you send during a fee dispute: itemized bill requests, demand letters,
discharge letters, and the rest.

## The distinction that matters

Proof of mailing is not proof of receipt. A mailing receipt shows you handed something to the
Postal Service on a given date. It does not show the other side got it. If receipt matters,
which it usually does when you are about to set a deadline in a demand letter, choose a method
that produces a receipt or a delivery record, not just a mailing record.

A refused or unclaimed certified letter is still useful. USPS lets an addressee refuse a piece
when it is offered for delivery. That refusal, and the returned envelope, are evidence the
letter was sent and that delivery was attempted. A lawyer who refuses certified mail from a
client has not made the dispute disappear.

## Methods

### Certified Mail alone

Proof: a mailing receipt and USPS tracking. Tracking shows when the piece was delivered, or
that a delivery attempt was made.

What it does not show: what was inside the envelope, or that anyone read it.

Worth it for: routine correspondence where you want a paper trail of when you sent something,
without paying for a signature.

### Certified Mail with Return Receipt (green card, or the electronic version)

Proof: everything Certified Mail gives you, plus a record of the recipient's signature. The
paper "green card" comes back to you by mail. The electronic version is emailed or downloaded
as a PDF instead.

What it does not show: what was inside the envelope. It shows who signed, not that the person
who signed is the lawyer personally, if someone else at the firm accepted it.

Worth it for: any letter where you may later need to prove the firm received it on a specific
date, which is most of the templates in this kit.

### Priority Mail with Signature Confirmation

Proof: date and time of delivery, or of a delivery attempt, plus the recipient's signature.
Similar in substance to Certified Mail with Return Receipt, on a faster mail class.

What it does not show: same limits as above.

Worth it for: the same situations as Certified Mail with Return Receipt, when you also want the
faster delivery speed of Priority Mail.

### Email

Proof: your own sent copy, with headers, showing the date, the address you sent it to, and the
content.

What it does not show: that the message was opened or read. Read receipts depend on the
recipient's mail client honoring the request, and most people never see the prompt or decline
it. Do not rely on a read receipt as your proof.

Worth it for: routine back-and-forth, and as a second channel alongside a mailed letter. A sent
email plus a later follow-up that references the earlier one, "as I wrote on [date]," is
evidence the first message went out and was not disputed at the time.

### Hand delivery with a witness

Proof: a signed, dated statement from the person who went with you, describing what was handed
over and to whom.

What it does not show: anything, without that statement. Hand delivery with no witness and no
receipt proves nothing after the fact.

Worth it for: a local firm, when you want delivery confirmed the same day and are willing to
bring someone along and write down what happened immediately afterward.

### Delivery to the law office in person

Proof: whatever the firm gives you. Some firms will stamp or sign a copy of your letter as
received if you ask. Many will not.

What it does not show: anything, if you leave without a receipt. Ask for one before you leave.
If the front desk declines, note the date, time, and who you spoke with, right after you leave.

## What this kit recommends

Send anything that matters, the demand letter especially, by more than one method. USPS mail
can be refused or go unclaimed. Email can be ignored or claimed to have gone to spam. Two
independent channels are harder to dismiss than one.

The default the templates use: Certified Mail with Return Receipt (electronic is fine) as the
primary method, plus a copy by email the same day. That combination is reflected in the sender
line of `templates/04_demand_letter.md`. If you can hand deliver with a witness as well, that is
a reasonable third channel for the demand letter specifically, not something to do for every
letter in the dispute.

## Email specifics

Keep your sent copy with full headers, not a screenshot. See `docs/RECORDS_RETENTION.md` for
why. If you send a follow-up, reference the date of the earlier message by name. That reference,
sitting in a later email the recipient does not dispute, is itself evidence the earlier one was
sent and received.
