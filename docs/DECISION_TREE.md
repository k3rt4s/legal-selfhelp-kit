# Decision tree

Answer in order. Stop when you reach an action.

## 1. Is money missing from a trust account, or did the lawyer take a retainer and do nothing at all?

Yes: this is potential dishonest conduct, not a fee dispute. File with the Board of Professional Responsibility and read the Client Protection Fund section in `references/tennessee-remedies.md`. Get a lawyer if the amount is significant.

No: continue.

## 2. Is the lawyer still representing you in an active matter?

Yes: decide whether you are ending the representation before you escalate. A dispute with your current lawyer in the middle of a live case can hurt you. Consider whether you have successor counsel lined up. Then continue.

No: continue.

## 3. Have you asked, in writing, for an itemized bill and an explanation?

No: do that first. Use `templates/01_itemized_bill_request.md`. Give fourteen days. Almost nothing downstream works without this step.

Yes, and they answered: continue to 4.

Yes, and they ignored you: after ten days with no response, contact the Consumer Assistance Program, which will follow up with the lawyer. Then continue to 4.

## 4. What is the core problem?

**The amount is too high, or I was charged for things I did not agree to.**

Go to 5.

**I cannot get my file or my documents.**

Send `templates/03_file_request.md`. RPC 1.16(d) requires the lawyer to surrender papers and property you are entitled to. If ignored, contact the Consumer Assistance Program.

**I paid in advance and the work was not done.**

Send `templates/02_unearned_fee_refund.md`. If refused and the pattern suggests no good faith intention to perform, read the Client Protection Fund section.

**They will not talk to me, but the bill is fine.**

This is an RPC 1.4 communication problem. Written request first, then the Consumer Assistance Program.

**Something dishonest happened.**

Board complaint. Go to 7.

## 5. Does your county have a fee dispute committee?

Programs exist in or around Memphis, Knoxville, Chattanooga, Bristol, Kingsport, Montgomery County, and Washington County. Confirm with your local bar association, because these vary and change.

Yes: contact the local bar association and ask how to submit. This is usually the cheapest path. Note that participation may require both sides to agree.

No: go to 6.

## 6. Is the disputed amount within the General Sessions limit?

The limit is $25,000 in most counties and $15,000 in Anderson, Davidson, Hamilton, and Knox. Confirm with the clerk.

Yes: send `templates/04_demand_letter.md` with a firm deadline. If it is refused, file in General Sessions in the right county.

No: the amount is beyond small claims. Talk to a lawyer about circuit court.

## 7. Do you want the conduct on the record regardless of money?

Yes: file with the Board. Use `templates/05_board_complaint_narrative.md` to build the narrative. Choose "formal complaint" only if you are alleging a serious ethical violation. If your real issue is the size of a bill, choose the informal request for assistance instead, because a billing disagreement filed as a formal complaint usually ends in a closed file.

No: you are done with the escalation path. Focus on the money route you picked above.
